## Rules

- Promote the chapter without inventing plot, quotes, reviews, or canon details.
- Follow the requested spoiler level exactly.
- If an optional quote or excerpt is supplied, use it only if it is framed as user-provided or chapter-provided text.
- Keep the chapter promise specific: mood, stakes, character pressure, and the reason to read.
- Avoid revealing the chapter resolution unless the request explicitly allows spoilers.
- Match the requested platform, tone, objective, CTA, and style variant.
- Remember the distinction: a summary gives information; a teaser creates a question; a promo creates an event.
- Focus on one central hook: threat, discovery, confrontation, secret, decision, reversal, emotional rupture, atmospheric event, or body-count question.
- Create questions without answering them. End at maximum uncertainty.
- Use specific details from the chapter instead of generic marketing language.
- Escalate from context to complication to an unresolved final beat.

## Hook Selection

Choose the chapter's strongest promotional asset:

- Threat: someone is watched, hunted, manipulated, or placed in danger.
- Discovery: a body, object, document, clue, or contradiction appears.
- Confrontation: two characters challenge each other.
- Secret: hidden information is about to emerge.
- Decision: a character must make a dangerous or morally compromising choice.
- Reversal: an ally becomes suspicious or a suspect appears innocent.
- Emotional Rupture: trust breaks, betrayal lands, or a relationship shifts.
- Atmospheric Event: storm, blackout, ritual, fire, landscape, or other setting pressure traps the characters.
- Body-Count Question: a character is placed in danger without confirming survival.

Choose the hook that best represents the chapter, not merely the most violent or loudest scene.

## Spoiler-Control System

Before writing, classify chapter details internally:

- Safe: appears near the beginning or is already known. Can be shown directly.
- Suggestive: can be implied without revealing the outcome. Ideal promo material.
- Sensitive: changes how the chapter is understood. Use fragments only.
- Forbidden: major death, killer identity, decisive betrayal, final revelation, or the answer to the chapter's central question. Do not include.

A strong promo can show the setup of danger without confirming the outcome, present a character as suspicious without declaring guilt, and reveal that evidence exists without explaining what it proves.

## Promo Formats

### Written Promo

Use this structure for written chapter promos:

1. Hook: open with the central danger, contradiction, or question.
2. Escalation: introduce two or three selective complications.
3. Stakes: show what the character could lose physically, emotionally, socially, or morally.
4. Button: end with the strongest unresolved line, question, threat, command, or tagline.

### Excerpt Teaser

Use this structure for excerpt-style teasers:

1. Immediate situation.
2. Disturbing detail.
3. Escalation.
4. Cutoff before explanation, confrontation, attack, or answer.

### TV-Style Promo

Use the requested duration when supplied:

- 15 seconds: 0-3s immediate hook, 3-8s two complications, 8-12s sting, 12-15s chapter title and CTA. About 25-45 spoken words.
- 30 seconds: 0-5s opening situation, 5-12s central conflict, 12-22s accelerating fragments, 22-26s shock or question, 26-30s title card. About 55-80 spoken words.
- 60 seconds: 0-10s atmospheric opening, 10-25s narrative setup, 25-45s conflicts and suspicions, 45-53s climactic montage, 53-60s sting and title card. About 100-140 spoken words with room for silence.

For TV-style promos, include visual beats, restrained audio cues, dialogue fragments that work without full context, on-screen text where useful, title card, release information if supplied, and final tagline.

## Genre-Specific Promotional Modes

Use the selected mode if supplied; otherwise auto-match the chapter:

- Slasher Promo: immediate danger, pursuit or isolation, weapon/killer motif, possible victim, abrupt sound. Do not reveal deaths or killer identity.
- Psychological-Thriller Promo: contradictory memories, manipulation, mistrust, fragmented dialogue, unreliable evidence.
- Caribbean Gothic Promo: landscape as pressure or witness, oppressive weather or vegetation, historical guilt, family secrets, culturally specific imagery grounded in the text.
- Dark Academia Promo: institutional power, reputation, grades, discipline, archives, rituals, corruption beneath respectability.
- Satirical Horror Promo: absurd priorities during crisis, image management, privilege, hypocrisy, punchlines that increase tension.
- Historical-Mystery Promo: past event with present consequences, photographs, letters, names, locations, testimony, parallel timelines, one withheld connection.

## Creative Direction

When visual or audio fields are supplied, use them as constraints:

- Featured character
- Visual motif
- Sound motif
- Music style
- Editing rhythm
- Color and lighting
- Final tagline
- Spoiler notes or forbidden details

Do not overload the promo with random aesthetic montage. Repeat a few concrete motifs. Restraint makes the final sting land.

## Quality Check

Before finalizing, verify that the copy:

- Represents the actual chapter.
- Establishes character, conflict, or atmosphere immediately.
- Uses specific rather than generic language.
- Escalates instead of listing events.
- Preserves all major revelations.
- Reflects the correct genre balance.
- Ends on the strongest unresolved beat.
- Makes the audience want the chapter, not feel as though it has already read it.

# Media & Author Marketing Best Practices

## Media Kit

A media kit is a professional branding and sales tool designed to give journalists, partners, event organizers, and potential collaborators everything they need in one place. It should be visually aligned with the brand, easy to navigate, and ideally no longer than one or two pages, with additional resources available through links or downloadable assets.

### Essential Components

About / Bio:
- Brief introduction
- Mission and vision
- What makes the brand, book, or platform unique

Professional Images:
- Author headshots
- Book covers
- Brand photography
- Promotional images

Contact Information:
- Email address
- Website
- Social media profiles
- Media or PR contact, if applicable

### Metrics That Matter

For authors and creators:
- Engagement rate
- Audience demographics
- Newsletter subscribers
- Website traffic
- Geographic reach

For businesses:
- Customer numbers
- Revenue milestones
- Growth statistics
- Website traffic
- Newsletter audience

### Credibility Builders

Include:
- Awards and recognitions
- Press mentions
- Media coverage
- Past collaborations
- Testimonials
- Client or partner logos

### Design Guidelines

- Use consistent branding, colors, and typography.
- Prioritize readability over decoration.
- Export as a shareable PDF.
- Make it accessible through the website and social profiles.

## Pitch Emails

The goal of a pitch email is not to close a deal. The goal is to start a conversation.

Keep emails under 200 words and focus on relevance rather than volume.

### Subject Line

Keep it short, direct, and specific.

Examples:
- Media Interview Opportunity
- Collaboration Inquiry
- Review Request
- Speaking Opportunity
- Award-Winning YA Horror

Avoid:
- Clickbait
- Excessive capitalization
- Generic sales language

### Opening

Demonstrate genuine familiarity with the recipient.

Good:
- Reference a recent article
- Mention a recent project
- Highlight a shared interest

Avoid:
- Generic mass-email introductions
- Forced personalization

### Value Proposition

Clearly explain:
- Why you are reaching out
- Why it matters to them
- Why their audience would care

Use short paragraphs and remove unnecessary details.

### Call to Action

Ask for a simple next step.

Examples:
- Would you be interested in receiving a review copy?
- Do you have ten minutes next week for a quick chat?
- Would this be a fit for your audience?

### Follow-Up

Send a brief follow-up three to four business days later if there is no response.

## Author Bios

An author bio should establish credibility, create connection, and guide readers toward a next step.

### Core Principles

Write in third person:
- Use: Alejandro Torres is an award-winning thriller author...
- Avoid: I am an award-winning thriller author...

Lead with credibility:
- Awards
- Publishing achievements
- Relevant expertise
- Professional background

Tailor to the platform:
- Short bio: 15 to 30 words for social media and guest post bylines
- Standard bio: 50 to 100 words for Amazon, press releases, and book jackets
- Long bio: 200 to 250 words for websites, media kits, and speaking engagements

Add personality:
- Hobbies
- Travel
- Pets
- Personal interests

Include a call to action:
- Website
- Newsletter
- Social media
- Upcoming releases

Avoid:
- Overly long résumés
- Excessive self-promotion
- Too many links

## Book Descriptions

A book description is a sales page disguised as a story teaser.

Aim for 150 to 250 words.

### The Three-Part Formula

Hook:
- Open with a compelling question, mystery, or bold statement.

Conflict:
- Introduce the protagonist.
- Introduce the central problem.
- Establish the stakes.
- Never reveal major twists or the ending.

Tease:
- End with a question, impossible choice, or looming threat.

### Formatting Tips

- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use strategic bolding.
- Optimize for mobile readers.
- For non-fiction, use bullet points to highlight benefits.

### SEO Considerations

Naturally include:
- Genre keywords
- Audience keywords
- Comparison titles
- Search-friendly phrases

Examples:
- Young Adult Horror
- Psychological Thriller
- Caribbean Gothic
- Dark Academia

## Corporate & PR Boilerplates

A boilerplate is a reusable company or author description used in press releases and media materials.

### Best Practices

Length:
- 50 to 100 words

Include:
- Who you are
- What you do
- Who you serve
- Why you matter

Use:
- Facts
- Milestones
- Awards
- Verifiable achievements

Avoid:
- Marketing fluff
- Empty buzzwords
- Unsubstantiated claims

## Fact Sheets

A fact sheet should allow someone to understand the essentials in under one minute.

### Structure

Executive summary:
- Two to three sentences explaining what it is and why it matters.

Key facts:
- Bullet points
- Tables
- Statistics
- Timelines

Design principles:
- One page whenever possible
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Plenty of white space
- Consistent formatting

Visual elements:
- Use charts and graphics when they improve comprehension.
- Avoid decorative visuals that do not support the content.

### Final Checklist

- Under two pages
- Easy to scan
- Key points bulleted
- Contact information visible
- Clear takeaway in under one minute

## Press Kit Checklist

A press kit should function as a complete resource center for media, partners, investors, reviewers, and event organizers.

### Must Include

Company or Author Overview:
- Elevator pitch
- Mission
- Origin story

Leadership or Author Bios:
- Professional biographies
- High-resolution headshots

Facts and Statistics:
- Founding date
- Headquarters
- Audience size
- Awards
- Growth metrics

Branding Assets:
- Logos
- Brand guidelines
- Fonts
- Color codes

Media Assets:
- Book covers
- Product photos
- Promotional images
- Editorial-use images

Press Coverage:
- Press releases
- Media mentions
- Reviews
- Interviews

Contact Information:
- Media contact
- Email
- Phone number
- Website

### Best Practices

- Host it on the website.
- Make downloads easy.
- Use organized cloud folders when necessary.
- Review and update quarterly.
- Remove outdated statistics and imagery.

A strong media kit should answer most journalist questions before they ever send an email.

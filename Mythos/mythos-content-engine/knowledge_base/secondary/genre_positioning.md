### **1\. Dark Academia (The Setting & Structure)**

* **The Trap:** Traditional Dark Academia relies on European architecture, cold New England weather, and elitist intellectualism (e.g., *The Secret History*).  
* **The Positioning:** *Mortal Vengeance* subverts this completely. It takes place at **Excelsior Academy**, a prestigious, strict Jesuit school in Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic. The narrative frames the institution not as a place of enlightenment, but as a "cathedral of repression" where authority figures weaponize Catholic guilt, mandatory confessions, and public humiliation to protect reputation and enforce absolute silence.

### **2\. Caribbean Gothic & Folk Horror (The Atmosphere)**

* **The Trap:** Tropical horror is often reduced to "islands in peril" or tourist-slasher tropes.  
* **The Positioning:** This is authentic, regional Gothic dread rooted in internal cultural realities rather than external exoticism. It uses the heavy, blistering tropical heat as a claustrophobic psychological stressor. Furthermore, it introduces **El Grim Cojuelo** (reimagining the traditional Dominican Carnival trickster devil, *El Cojuelo*, into a terrifying, skeletal hunter of the guilty). The entity functions simultaneously as a supernatural entity and a psychological manifestation of collective shame, trauma, and inherited systemic rot.

### **3\. Mature YA / New Adult Psychological Thriller (The Tone)**

* **The Trap:** Many young adult thrillers lean into light-hearted mysteries or soften their themes with heavy romance.  
* **The Positioning:** This series has a sharp, cinematic, and unflinching psychological edge, earning its 18+ recommendation. It deals head-on with heavy themes like institutional grooming, severe trauma, self-harm, and intense familial pressure (such as the raw exploration of LGBTQ+ identity and survival within deeply conservative environments).

## **What It Is**

*Mortal Vengeance* is:

**Dark academia**, because Excelsior Academy is not just a school. It is a pressure cooker of status, secrets, tradition, silence, and reputation.

**Slasher horror**, because the Grim Cojuelo functions as a masked figure of terror, vengeance, and consequence.

**Psychological thriller**, because the real engine is guilt, manipulation, paranoia, social pressure, and the collapse of trust.

**Caribbean Gothic**, because the horror is rooted in Santo Domingo, Dominican identity, Catholic-school culture, folklore, heat, class, family, and social silence.

**Meta horror**, because the series understands slasher language and plays with performance, spectacle, media, evidence, and audience gaze.

**Found-footage / reality-TV horror**, especially as the series expands, because cameras, confessionals, recovered media, B-roll, and broadcast framing become part of the storytelling machinery.

## **What It Is Not**

Do **not** position it primarily as fantasy.

Do **not** position it as supernatural horror first.

Do **not** call the Grim Cojuelo a literal demon if the canon treats him as a human-made, folklore-rooted role.

Do **not** reduce it to “YA mystery.” That is beige. Beige is where genre positioning goes to die wearing sensible shoes.

The Grim Cojuelo is stronger when framed as an **occupiable antagonist role**: a culturally rooted mask someone can step into, rather than a single monster or ghost. That is one of the series’ most original genre assets.

# **0\. A Note on Genre & Scope**

This file consolidates prior competitor passes into one working reference. The comp set straddles YA and adult — but that is not a problem to resolve, it is a feature of the series to position around. Mortal Vengeance escalates across installments: A Grim Tale (prequel) and Book 1 read as mature-YA / crossover, while MV II (\~255k words; institutional grooming, systemic violence, on-page brutality) reads as adult / upmarket. A series that ages up with its readers is a recognized and saleable shape — the entry point recruits, the later books retain and deepen.

**This produces a deliberate two-tier comp map, not a muddle:**

* **Entry point (A Grim Tale / Book 1\) — the “recruit” shelf.** Mature-YA / crossover. This is where discovery happens: McManus, Holly Jackson, Àbíké-Íyímídé, Dávila Cardinal, Moldavsky. These comps do the acquisition work and set the BookTok / book-club entry register.

* **MV II onward — the “elevate” shelf.** Adult / upmarket. This is where the ceiling is signaled and readers are retained as the material darkens: Stephen Graham Jones, Silvia Moreno-Garcia, Donna Tartt, R.F. Kuang, Isabel Cañas.

Practical consequence: YA comps anchor the back-cover “for fans of” and early-funnel marketing; adult comps anchor agent/festival pitches and reviewer targeting for the later books. The tables below tag each comp **A** (adult), **YA**, or **X** (crossover) so the recruit-vs-elevate split stays visible at a glance.

# **1\. Where Mortal Vengeance Sits**

MV occupies a four-way intersection that no single competitor fully owns:

* **Dark academia** — elite-school setting, institutional secrets, intellectual dread (The Secret History lineage, very active 2024–2026).

* **Meta / self-aware slasher** — the Scream tradition in prose, now a genuine literary moment.

* **Reality-TV / found-footage horror** — the diegetic camera and broadcast dossier as weapon.

* **Caribbean / Latine gothic** — folklore-rooted horror from a specific cultural geography (Dominican, bilingual).

**Thesis:** each competitor owns one or two of these lanes. **Mortal Vengeance owns the collision.** Three structural signatures seal it, none of which competitors carry together: the Grim Cojuelo as an occupiable antagonist role (a human-made myth, not a supernatural monster), the Excelsior Archive / B-roll dossier format, and the Hybrid Cinematic tense system.

# **5\. Positioning Gaps (The Whitespace)**

* **“Dark academia in the sun.”** DA defaults to cold, Euro/New-England settings. A sun-drenched, claustrophobic Jesuit academy in Santo Domingo — heat itself as a psychological stressor — is almost entirely unclaimed.

* **Reality-TV slasher \+ dark academia, fused.** Patricia Wants to Cuddle has the reality frame but no academia; The Four has the academia but no media frame. The fusion is open.

* **Slasher (not ghost story) Latine horror.** Latine horror skews atmospheric haunting / magical realism (La Llorona, hauntings). A visceral, masked-killer slasher serves Scream fans wanting Latine representation — a demographic the “quiet” end underserves.

* **Society and complicity over monster horror.** The terror isn’t a ghost haunting the school — it’s that class-protected silence creates the conditions a person can step into the Grim Cojuelo role to exploit. The horror is social and human-made: an occupiable role, not a creature, and not confined to the school as setting. Competitor-free.

* **Religious-trauma × slasher.** Catholic-school critique usually arrives via rom-com/coming-of-age (e.g. The Lesbiana’s Guide to Catholic School). Doing it through horror is a high-interest, low-competition niche.

* **Bilingual prose as a feature.** Most comps are English-default with Spanish seasoning. Structurally bilingual Spanish/English prose differentiates and opens a second market few peers contest.

* **Spanish-language expansion lane.** No prominent YA/crossover Caribbean Gothic series is actively marketed in Spanish to Latin America / Spain. Given a Barcelona base and bilingual capability, this is a concrete, low-competition lane.

* **The “no fluff” mature edge.** Reviewers note MV doesn’t tiptoe around heavy material. That captures readers who’ve aged out of soft YA but still want youthful protagonists — the mature-YA / NA seam.

*Mortal Vengeance* stands out in a crowded YA/Thriller market because of three highly specific elements:

## **4\. Positioning Gaps & Market Opportunities**

Analyzing the current layout of the thriller market reveals key gaps that *Mortal Vengeance* is perfectly built to exploit:

* **The "Dark Academia in the Sun" Void:** Most Dark Academia relies on cold, wintery settings. There is a massive market gap for tropical/subtropical Gothic dread (leveraging the oppressive heat as a psychological stressor), which this series anchors perfectly.  
* **The "No Fluff" YA Thriller Demand:** Many YA thrillers pull their punches or lean heavily into romantic subplots to soften the blow. Reviewers specifically highlight that *Mortal Vengeance* "does not tiptoe around heavy material" (trauma, institutional grooming, systemic negligence). It sits comfortably in the *New Adult* or *Mature YA* edge, capturing readers who feel they have outgrown traditional YA but still want youthful protagonists.  
* **Systemic Horror Over Monster Horror:** The market has a surplus of simple supernatural monsters. Position the series heavily on the fact that *the institution itself creates the monster*. The terror isn't that a ghost is haunting the school; it's that the school's silence summoned the executioner.

3\. Campaigns & Marketing Notes

Analysis of the [*Mortal Vengeance*](https://www.google.com/search?q=mortal+vengeance&kgmid=/hkb/Cg4KCGxhbmd1YWdlEgJlbgoMCgR0eXBlEgRCT09LCh8KC2VudGl0eV9uYW1lEhBtb3J0YWwgdmVuZ2VhbmNl&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjamuPN9u2UAxUXKvsDHb72CYkQ3egRegoIAggACAEICxAD) marketing vs. industry standards.

* Transmedia "B-Roll" Archive (Unique Selling Point):  
  * *Mortal Vengeance* employs a "B-Roll Archive" concept—dossiers of "recovered media," fake news transcripts (CNN, BBC), and police files to blur fiction and reality.  
  * Comparison: This mimics "Alternate Reality Game" (ARG) marketing used by major horror franchises (e.g., *Cloverfield*, *Longlegs*). It elevates the series above standard Indie thrillers by offering immersive lore.  
* "Caribbean Gothic" Branding:  
  * The series leans heavily into the specific aesthetic of "Sun-drenched Noir"—horror that happens in the heat of the Caribbean rather than the fog of London.  
  * Competitor Strategy: *Mexican Gothic* used this successfully (green dresses, rotting mansions). *Mortal Vengeance* adapts this for a younger, slasher-focused audience (carnival masks, school courtyards).  
* Indie vs. Trad Tactics:  
  * Competitors like [*Ace of Spades*](https://www.google.com/search?q=ace+of+spades&kgmid=/g/11k6g4rjgx&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjamuPN9u2UAxUXKvsDHb72CYkQ3egRegoIAggACAEIDBAO) rely on "High Concept" pitches (e.g., "Gossip Girl meets Get Out") and BookTok trends.  
  * *Mortal Vengeance* appears to utilize grassroots indie tactics (Newsletters, IndieReader reviews, Literary Titan awards) rather than viral TikTok "tropes" marketing.

4\. Positioning Gaps

Where [*Mortal Vengeance*](https://www.google.com/search?q=mortal+vengeance&kgmid=/hkb/Cg4KCGxhbmd1YWdlEgJlbgoMCgR0eXBlEgRCT09LCh8KC2VudGl0eV9uYW1lEhBtb3J0YWwgdmVuZ2VhbmNl&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjamuPN9u2UAxUXKvsDHb72CYkQ3egRegoIAggACAEIDhAD) sits in the market relative to competitors.

* The "Tropical Slasher" Gap:  
  * Most "Dark Academia" is Euro-centric (Vermont, UK, ancient universities).  
  * *Gap:* There is a lack of Dark Academia set in the Caribbean. *Mortal Vengeance* fills this by placing the "elite corrupt school" trope in Santo Domingo, contrasting "First World" academic prestige with "Third World" political corruption/violence.  
* Folklore vs. Ghost Story:  
  * Latinx horror often leans into "Ghost Stories" (La Llorona, hauntings).  
  * *Gap:* *Mortal Vengeance* positions itself closer to the Slasher genre (visceral violence, masked killer "El Grim Cojuelo") rather than atmospheric haunting. This appeals to fans of *Scream* who want Latinx representation, a demographic often underserved by "quiet" magical realism.  
* The "Jesuit/Religious" Critique:  
  * While books like *The Lesbiana's Guide to Catholic School* critique Catholic education from a rom-com/coming-of-age angle, *Mortal Vengeance* critiques it through Horror. This specific intersection (Religious Trauma x Slasher Horror) is a high-interest niche with low direct competition.

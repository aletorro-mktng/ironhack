# Podcast Script Best Practices

A strong podcast script should sound natural, conversational, and easy to perform. The goal is not to write something that feels perfectly polished on the page. The goal is to create a script that sounds alive when spoken.

## 1. Write for the Spoken Word

Podcast scripts should be written for listening, not silent reading.

### Best Practices

* **Use conversational language.** Avoid academic phrasing, stiff transitions, or overly formal sentences.
* **Use contractions.** Write “don’t,” “can’t,” and “we’re” instead of “do not,” “cannot,” and “we are.”
* **Keep sentences short.** Long sentences are harder to deliver naturally.
* **Write the way people speak.** If a line feels awkward out loud, rewrite it.
* **Write out numbers and symbols.** Use “twelve hundred” instead of “1,200” when it helps the speaker avoid stumbling.
* **Read the script aloud before recording.** This catches clunky phrasing, awkward pacing, and sentences that look fine but sound like they escaped from a tax document.

A useful estimate: one minute of spoken audio is roughly 125 to 150 words.

## 2. Use a Clear Episode Structure

A podcast episode should feel guided, not improvised into the wilderness.

### Recommended Structure

#### Hook

Open with a strong hook in the first 15-30 seconds.

Use:

* A compelling question.
* A bold statement.
* A surprising fact.
* A short story.
* A dramatic moment.
* A clear promise of what the listener will get.

#### Show Introduction

Briefly introduce:

* The podcast name.
* The host.
* The episode topic.
* Why the episode matters.

Keep this section tight. The listener came for the episode, not a ceremonial ribbon-cutting.

#### Main Body

Deliver the core content in clear segments.

Use sections for:

* Main arguments.
* Story beats.
* Interview questions.
* Examples.
* Commentary.
* Lessons or takeaways.

Each segment should connect naturally to the next.

#### Outro & Call-to-Action

Close with:

* A quick summary of the main takeaway.
* A thank-you to listeners or guests.
* One clear call-to-action.

Examples:

* Subscribe to the podcast.
* Leave a review.
* Visit the website.
* Read a related article.
* Join the newsletter.
* Share the episode.

Use one primary CTA whenever possible.

## 3. Add Delivery Notes

Podcast scripts should help the speaker perform, not trap them in tiny text prison.

Use performance notes such as:

* **[Pause]**
* **[Beat]**
* **[Emphasize]**
* **[Laugh]**
* **[Slow down]**
* **[Shift tone]**
* **[Ad-lib if natural]**

These notes should guide delivery without cluttering the script.

## 4. Add Production Notes

Include audio and editing cues where needed.

Examples:

* **[Intro music fades in]**
* **[Intro music fades out]**
* **[Sound effect: phone notification]**
* **[Insert clip]**
* **[Sponsor break]**
* **[Transition music]**
* **[Fade to silence]**

Production notes help the final episode feel intentional and polished.

## 5. Format for Easy Reading

The script should be easy to scan while recording.

Use:

* Short paragraphs.
* Clear section headings.
* Line breaks between ideas.
* Bold text for emphasis.
* Bullet points for lists.
* Speaker labels for multi-host or interview formats.

Avoid dense blocks of text. If the host loses their place mid-recording, the script has failed its one job.

## 6. Keep the Tone Authentic

A podcast should sound like a real person talking to real listeners.

Use:

* Natural transitions.
* Personal commentary.
* Specific examples.
* Occasional humor when appropriate.
* Emotional pacing.
* Direct address to the listener.

Avoid generic filler, robotic phrasing, and over-scripted “radio announcer” energy unless that is the intentional format.

## 7. Quality Checklist

Before recording, check:

* Does the opening hook work within the first 30 seconds?
* Does the episode have a clear structure?
* Does the script sound natural when read aloud?
* Are numbers and difficult terms easy to say?
* Are production cues clear?
* Is there one clear CTA?
* Does the ending feel complete?
* Would someone keep listening after the first minute?

A good podcast script should give the host confidence, give the editor clarity, and give the listener a reason to stay.

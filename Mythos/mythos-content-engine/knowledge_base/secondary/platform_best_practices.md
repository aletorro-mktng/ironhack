

# **Content Announcement Best Practices**

To make announcements, podcasts, and blog posts more effective, the content should be clear, human, structured, and easy to reuse across platforms.

## **Press Release Best Practices**

A strong press release should communicate the news immediately and give journalists everything they need without making them dig through corporate fog.

### **Lead with a clear headline**

The headline should be short, objective, and newsworthy. Ideally, keep it under 100 characters. Use strong action verbs and make the main announcement obvious from the start.

Weak:  
“We Are Thrilled to Announce an Exciting New Milestone”

Stronger:  
“Mortal Vengeance Wins IndieReader Discovery Award for YA Horror”

### **Answer the 5 Ws immediately**

The first paragraph should explain:

Who is involved  
What happened  
When it happened  
Where it happened  
Why it matters

A journalist should understand the full story within the first few lines.

### **Use authentic quotes**

Avoid empty corporate phrases like “We are thrilled to announce.” Quotes should add personality, perspective, or emotional context. They should explain why the news matters, not repeat facts already stated in the release.

### **Support claims with facts**

Use numbers, dates, awards, rankings, sales data, audience reach, or industry context when available. Specific details make the announcement feel credible and give journalists stronger material to work with.

### **Keep it concise**

Aim for under 500 words. Use short paragraphs and bullet points where helpful. A press release should be easy to scan, not a hostage situation in Times New Roman.

### **Include a boilerplate**

End with a short “About” section that gives instant context about the author, company, book, or organization.

### **Provide multimedia**

Include high-quality images, logos, book covers, award badges, author photos, or infographics when relevant. Add clear captions so publishers can use them easily.

# **AI Podcast Best Practices**

AI-generated podcasts work best when the script sounds natural, the voices are distinct, and the final audio is reviewed by human ears.

## **1\. Master the Script and Flow**

Written text does not automatically sound natural when spoken aloud. A podcast script should be written for listening, not reading.

Use shorter sentences, active voice, and conversational phrasing. Avoid corporate jargon, clinical language, and overly polished dialogue that makes the hosts sound like two LinkedIn posts trapped in a recording booth.

For a two-host format, define clear roles. For example:

One host can be the expert who provides context.  
The other can be the curious beginner who asks questions, reacts naturally, and keeps the discussion accessible.

Natural conversational markers can help, but they should be used lightly. Small pauses, brief interruptions, and casual phrasing can make the audio feel more human. Too much filler makes it sound like the hosts are trapped in a malfunctioning elevator.

## **2\. Choose and Tune the AI Voices**

The voice style should match the topic. A casual entertainment podcast can use a warmer, more energetic tone. A business or news-focused podcast should feel grounded, clear, and authoritative.

For multi-voice podcasts, choose voices with different pitch, rhythm, and personality. If both voices sound too similar, listeners may lose track of who is speaking.

Control pacing and pauses. Good audio needs breathing room between ideas, especially when moving from one topic to another.

## **3\. Polish the Audio**

AI can automate production, but the final version still needs human review.

Listen for:

Mispronounced names  
Awkward emphasis  
Robotic intonation  
Sudden tone shifts  
Unnatural pacing  
Confusing speaker transitions

If something sounds off, revise the script and regenerate that specific line instead of accepting bad audio because “the robot tried its best.” No participation trophy for weird pronunciation.

If using background music, keep narration clearly dominant. Music should support the voice, not wrestle it for custody of the listener’s ears.

## **4\. Repurpose the Podcast**

The podcast should become more than one audio file.

Use the transcript to create:

Short-form video clips  
Audiograms  
Social media posts  
Episode summaries  
Clickable timestamps  
YouTube descriptions  
Blog posts  
Newsletter blurbs

One episode can become a full content ecosystem if planned properly.

**Blog Post Best Practices**

A high-performing blog post should be easy to skim, useful to the reader, and optimized for search without sounding like it was assembled by an SEO goblin.

## **1\. Structure for Skimmability**

Online readers scan before they commit. Make the article visually easy to navigate.

Use:

Short paragraphs of 2–4 sentences  
Clear H2 and H3 headings  
Bullet points for key takeaways  
White space between sections  
Bold text only where it genuinely helps

Avoid dense walls of text. Nobody opens a blog post hoping to enter a literary basement.

## **2\. Start with a Strong Hook**

The introduction should immediately address the reader’s problem, question, or desired outcome.

A good opening tells the reader:

Why this matters  
What they will learn  
Why they should keep reading

Do not start with generic throat-clearing like “In today’s fast-paced digital world.” That sentence should be taken behind the barn and retired.

## **3\. Focus on One Main Goal**

Each post should have one clear purpose. Do not try to cover an entire industry in one article.

Stick to 2–3 focused ideas and develop them well. A focused post is more useful, easier to rank, and more likely to keep readers engaged.

## **4\. Use a Conversational Tone**

Write clearly and directly. Avoid academic or corporate language unless the audience specifically expects it.

The ideal tone should feel informed but approachable: smart enough to be credible, simple enough to be useful.

## **5\. Optimize for SEO**

Each blog post should target one primary keyword or topic. The title should ideally stay under 65–70 characters so it does not get cut off in search results.

Include:

A clear title  
Relevant headings  
Internal links to related content  
External links to credible sources  
A strong meta description  
Alt text for images

SEO should support the writing, not strangle it.

## **6\. Add Useful Visuals**

Images, infographics, charts, videos, or embedded clips can break up the text and add value. Visuals should clarify or enhance the article, not just decorate it.

## **7\. End with a Clear CTA**

Every post should tell the reader what to do next.

Examples:

Read a related article  
Subscribe to a newsletter  
Leave a comment  
Download a resource  
Listen to the podcast episode  
Share the announcement  
Buy the book

The CTA should match the purpose of the content. Don’t ask readers to do five things at once. That is not strategy. That is a digital tantrum.

# **Social Media Content Best Practices**

Creating strong TikToks, Instagram Reels, Posts, Carousels, and Stories requires a mix of platform-specific hooks, SEO, accessibility, and clear calls to action. The goal is simple: make content easy to find, easy to understand, and easy to share.

## **Instagram Posts & Carousels**

Instagram should be treated as a search engine, not just a visual platform. Captions, keywords, alt text, and hashtags all help the content get discovered.

### **Lead with searchable descriptions**

Start captions with clear subject matter instead of vague mood-setting.

Weak:  
“Sunday vibes ☕”

Stronger:  
“Minimalist coffee shop in Gràcia, Barcelona with quiet corners for remote work.”

The caption should quickly answer what the post is about, who it is for, and why someone should care.

### **Use the first 125 characters wisely**

Only the first part of the caption appears before the “more” button. Put the strongest hook, benefit, or story angle at the beginning.

Examples:

“Barcelona writers, save this café for your next editing session.”

“Here’s why this YA horror award matters more than just a badge.”

“Three things most authors get wrong when promoting a book online.”

### **Format for readability**

Avoid dense caption blocks. Use short paragraphs, line breaks, and occasional emojis when appropriate. The caption should feel skimmable, not like a hostage note from the algorithm.

### **Add strategic alt text**

Use Instagram’s advanced settings to add descriptive alt text. This helps accessibility and can also support visual search.

Example:

“Book cover for Mortal Vengeance beside an IndieReader Discovery Awards badge, styled as a dark YA horror promotional graphic.”

### **Use selective hashtags**

Use 5–15 highly relevant hashtags instead of maxing out at 30\. Prioritize niche and topic-specific tags over generic reach-bait.

Example:

\#YAHorror  
\#IndieAuthor  
\#BookMarketing  
\#DominicanWriters  
\#HorrorBooks  
\#AwardWinningBook

## **Instagram Reels**

Reels need to capture attention immediately. The first few seconds decide whether someone watches or scrolls away like they’re dodging a tax audit.

### **Open with a strong 3-second hook**

The opening frame should include a bold visual, clear text overlay, or direct statement that names the topic.

Examples:

“This YA horror book just won an award.”

“Your book promo is failing because it looks too generic.”

“Here’s how to turn one press release into ten pieces of content.”

### **Use text overlays strategically**

Put the most important idea on screen early. A short 1–2 word visual hook can work well on the cover or first frame.

Examples:

“AWARD WINNER”  
“BOOK MARKETING”  
“PRESS RELEASE”  
“CONTENT STRATEGY”

### **Write expandable captions**

Instagram allows longer Reel captions, but only the first two lines show before users tap “more.” Start with the strongest value proposition or story.

Example:

“Mortal Vengeance started as teenage revenge fantasy. Now it’s an award-winning YA horror novel.”

Then use the rest of the caption to add context, keywords, and a CTA.

### **Optimize captions for SEO**

Instagram indexes caption keywords, so describe the content clearly. Include the who, what, where, and why when relevant.

Example:

“YA horror author Alejandro Torres shares how Mortal Vengeance won the IndieReader Discovery Award for Best YA Horror and why Dominican folklore shaped the story.”

### **Add captions for sound-off viewing**

Many users watch videos without sound. Use native captions or manually edited subtitles so the video still makes sense on mute.

### **Keep it concise**

Although longer Reels are possible, 15–30 seconds often works best for completion and replay value. If the idea needs more time, split it into a series.

### **End with one clear CTA**

Give viewers one specific action.

Examples:

“Save this for your next launch.”  
“Comment GUIDE and I’ll send the checklist.”  
“Read the caption for the full breakdown.”  
“Share this with an author who needs it.”

Do not ask them to like, comment, save, share, subscribe, call their mother, and file quarterly taxes. One CTA. Clean. Sharp. Useful.

## **Instagram Stories**

Stories are best for immediacy, community interaction, and pushing people toward feed posts, Reels, links, or offers.

### **Keep text short and scannable**

Story text should be readable in 2–3 seconds. Keep overlays under 10 words when possible.

Examples:

“New award announcement.”  
“Behind the book cover.”  
“Vote: which promo works better?”  
“New Reel is live.”

Use high contrast text, such as white text on a dark background or text inside a solid box.

### **Use interactive stickers**

Polls, sliders, question boxes, quizzes, and “Add Yours” stickers help increase engagement.

Examples:

“Would you read this?”  
“Which cover wins?”  
“Ask me about publishing.”  
“Pick the stronger headline.”

### **Reshare feed posts and Reels with context**

Do not just dump a post into Stories and call it strategy. Add a reason to click.

Weak:  
New post.

Stronger:  
“This is the exact caption formula I’d use for a book award announcement.”

### **Add location tags and stickers**

Location tags can help local discovery, especially for events, bookstores, launches, cafés, travel content, or city-specific posts.

### **Use auto-captions for spoken Stories**

If you are speaking on camera, always add captions. Viewers watching on mute should still understand the message.

## **TikTok Posts**

TikTok is also a search engine. Strong captions, keywords, and hashtags help the platform understand who should see the video.

### **Use keyword-rich captions**

TikTok captions are short, so use 3–5 relevant keywords naturally.

Example:

“YA horror book marketing tips for indie authors.”

Or:

“How I turned Mortal Vengeance into an award-winning YA horror novel.”

### **Keep captions punchy**

TikTok captions should be fast, clear, and intriguing.

Examples:

“This started as revenge. It became a book.”  
“Book marketing lesson: stop hiding the story.”  
“Indie authors, this is your launch checklist.”  
“Would you read a Dominican YA horror novel?”

### **Use strategic hashtags**

Use 3–5 hashtags that combine broad discovery with niche relevance.

Example:

\#BookTok  
\#YAHorror  
\#IndieAuthor  
\#WritingCommunity  
\#BookMarketing

Avoid relying only on generic hashtags like \#fyp. That’s not a strategy. That’s throwing glitter into a hurricane.

### **Tag relevant creators or brands**

Tag featured creators, collaborators, publishers, award organizations, bookstores, or relevant brands when appropriate. This can improve discoverability and context.

## **Cross-Platform Best Practices**

Across TikTok, Reels, Posts, Carousels, and Stories, the same core principles apply:

Lead with the topic clearly.  
Make the first line or first frame count.  
Use captions and text overlays for accessibility.  
Write for search, not just aesthetics.  
Use hashtags selectively.  
Give the viewer one clear action.  
Repurpose strong content across formats.

One strong idea can become:

A TikTok  
A Reel  
A Carousel  
A Story sequence  
A YouTube Short  
A LinkedIn post  
A blog paragraph  
An email newsletter section

That is how content stops being a one-time post and becomes an actual marketing system.

## **BEST PRACTICES TO WRITE LINKEDIN ARTICLES**

To write a successful LinkedIn article, lead with a strong hook in your first paragraph, format for quick scanning using 2-3 sentence paragraphs, and deliver highly actionable insights. Target a word count between 800 to 1,500 words to establish authority and satisfy both readers and search engines. 

Maximize your reach and engagement with these proven best practices:

### 1\. Structure & Formatting

* Create a visual hierarchy: Use headings (H2 and H3) to break your piece into digestible sections.  
* Keep paragraphs short: Restrict paragraphs to two or three sentences. Walls of text lead to high bounce rates.  
* Use bulleted lists: Break down steps, takeaways, or data points into bullet points.  
* Include a cover image: Use a clean, relevant, high-resolution header photo (typically 744 × 400 pixels). 

### 2\. Content & Tone

* Deliver value upfront: Don't save your best advice for the end. Present the core problem, your proposed solution, and concrete takeaways early on.   
* Go deep, not generic: Avoid surface-level corporate speak. Share unique industry data, personal case studies, or actionable frameworks.   
* Write conversationally: Adopt a professional yet direct tone. Write as if you are sending a detailed email to a specific colleague.   
* Cite authoritative sources: Backing your claims with solid data or external references builds credibility and helps AI engines cite your content in search results. 

### 3\. Visibility & SEO

* Optimize for search: Fill out the article settings (accessible via the Settings menu) to create a custom URL, SEO title, and SEO description.   
* Use targeted keywords: Incorporate relevant search terms naturally throughout your title and text to tap into long-term organic discovery.   
* Add relevant hashtags: Tag your article with 3–4 specific hashtags when promoting it in your feed.

### 4\. Promotion & Engagement

* Add a Call-to-Action (CTA): Always end with a question or actionable prompt (e.g., "What has been your experience with X? Let me know in the comments.").   
* Repurpose in the feed: When you publish, create a native LinkedIn post linking to your new article. Summarize the biggest takeaway, and include the link in the comments or the post body.  
* Feature on your profile: Pin your best-performing articles to the Featured section on your profile so new visitors immediately see your expertise

Hook the reader in the first 3 lines with a bold statement, micro-story, or intriguing question before the "Read more" button. ### 3\. Driving Engagement & Visibility

* End with a specific CTA: Tell readers exactly what to do (e.g., *"What's your take on this?"* or *"Have you dealt with this challenge?"*).

## **BEST PRACTICES TO WRITE YOUTUBE VIDEO TITLES**

To write the best YouTube video titles, front-load your primary keyword in the first 3–5 words and keep the length under 55–60 characters so it doesn't get truncated on mobile devices. Pair the title with an honest, curiosity-inducing promise that triggers desire or fear. 

Follow these core best practices to drive higher click-through rates (CTR) and improve search visibility:

### 1\. Structure for Scannability

* Front-load keywords: Put the most important words and search terms on the left side, as people read from left to right.  
* Keep it short and simple: Aim for 50–60 characters. Shorter titles are faster to process in a split-second feed.  
* Limit ALL CAPS and Emojis: Use them selectively—only to emphasize strong emotions or special elements, rather than to scream at the viewer.  
* Use brackets or parentheses: Add brackets at the end of the title for context (e.g., \[Guide\], (2026), or \[Free Template\]). 

### 2\. Focus on Viewer Psychology

* Answer WIIFM (What's in it for my viewer?): Clearly communicate the outcome or value the viewer will gain from watching.   
* Trigger emotion and curiosity: Titles that perform best spark interest by tapping into a viewer's desires (e.g., success, saving time) or fears (e.g., missing out, making mistakes).   
* Use numbers and lists: Listicles and step-by-step guides (e.g., "5 Ways to...") are consistently successful because they signal structured, easy-to-digest content. 

### 3\. Algorithm and Content Alignment

* Use natural language: Avoid robotic keyword stuffing. Your title should read like a normal, engaging sentence.   
* Don't use misleading clickbait: While teasing information is encouraged, making false promises hurts audience retention, which in turn hurts YouTube's recommendation algorithm.   
* Coordinate with your thumbnail: Do not repeat the exact text in your thumbnail and title. Use the title to add the context, while the thumbnail grabs the immediate attention.

## **BEST PRACTICES TO WRITE YOUTUBE VIDEO DESCRIPTIONS**

YouTube video descriptions help algorithms categorize your content and guide viewers on what to do next. To maximize reach and engagement, write a 2-line hook, include your primary link above the fold, summarize the value naturally with keywords, format with chapters, and use upload defaults to streamline the process. 

### 1\. Optimize "Above the Fold" (First 2 Lines)

* The Hook: The first 1-2 lines are what viewers see before clicking "Show more," and they heavily influence search rankings. Start with a clear, conversational one-sentence summary of the video that uses your primary keyword.   
* Prioritize Links: Instead of a long summary, use the second line to drive the viewer to their next step. Place your primary Call-to-Action (CTA) here—such as a link to a lead magnet, a sponsor’s offer, or an affiliate link.   
* Avoid Keyword Stuffing: Write naturally for humans rather than trying to trick the algorithm. Use semantically related terms naturally. 

### 2\. Craft the Body

* Detailed Summary: In 3-4 sentences, clearly explain what the viewer will get out of the video. Naturally weave in secondary keywords.  
* Timestamps/Chapters: For videos longer than 5 minutes, use timestamps (e.g., 00:00 \- Intro). This makes the video skimmable and boosts discoverability on Google search results.  
* Next Steps: List links to your other relevant videos, playlists, social media, and website. 

### 3\. Maintain Professionalism and Structure

* Formatting: Break walls of text into short, readable paragraphs. You can use simple markdown on YouTube like capitalizing text or using symbols (such as \-) for bullet points. Use emojis sparingly to add personality and draw the eye.   
* Hashtags: Tack on 2–3 relevant hashtags at the very end of your description.   
* Upload Defaults: To save time, set up channel-wide description templates (like your social media links and standard disclaimers) using YouTube Studio Upload Defaults so you only need to edit the top section for each new video.

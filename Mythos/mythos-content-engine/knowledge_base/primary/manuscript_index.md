# Mortal Vengeance

## Book Position

Book 1

## Core Premise

At Excelsior Academy, an elite Catholic school in Santo Domingo, reputation is currency, silence is survival, and cruelty wears a polished uniform.

When a group of students decides to retaliate against a feared teacher whose abuse has gone unchecked for years, what begins as revenge quickly spirals into something far darker. Their choices bind them together through guilt, secrets, jealousy, and fear. Then a masked figure inspired by the Dominican legend of the Grim Cojuelo begins stalking the people connected to their sins.

As old friendships fracture and buried resentments come roaring back, the students are forced to confront a brutal question: if you witness injustice and do nothing, are you innocent, or are you part of the crime?

Mortal Vengeance is a Caribbean-set YA horror/slasher thriller about revenge, complicity, power, friendship, and the consequences of staying silent when cruelty becomes normal.

## Main Characters

 Alex Herrera

A charismatic, emotionally intense, manipulative student whose intelligence and charm make him dangerous. Alex is witty, strategic, jealous, and deeply afraid of abandonment. His feelings for Melissa and his need for control place him at the center of the group’s moral collapse.

Marcos Pérez

The angry protector whose hatred of injustice makes him both sympathetic and dangerous. Marcos begins with a real grievance against cruelty, but his rage becomes the spark that helps turn justice into revenge.

Mario Stinga

The anxious conscience of the group. Mario wants to belong, but he knows when things have gone too far. His guilt, fear, loyalty, and vulnerability make him one of the clearest emotional mirrors of the book’s central moral conflict.

Manuel Freites

The cynical observer with a wounded heart under layers of sarcasm. Manuel uses dark humor as armor, but the violence around him forces that armor to crack.

Fernando Pepino

Alex’s former best friend and one of the story’s most emotionally loaded figures. Stylish, provocative, hostile, and wounded, Fernando carries unresolved history with Alex that becomes crucial to the emotional architecture of the book.

Enrique Hartling

The polished golden boy whose desire to please makes him easy to manipulate. Enrique becomes entangled in the group’s revenge plot and in Alex’s jealousy because of his relationship with Melissa.

Melissa Rocha

The emotional center of the story. Kind, radiant, intelligent, and stronger than people assume, Melissa becomes the person onto whom others project love, obsession, guilt, and protection. Her arc moves her from idealized “dream girl” to active witness of the truth.

Mónika Torres

Stylish, sharp, theatrical, and socially powerful. Mónika brings fashion, wit, and camera-ready confidence to the group, but beneath the glamour she becomes one of the story’s key witnesses to fear, betrayal, and truth.

María García

Loyal, practical, brave, and protective. María is Melissa’s close friend and Lieutenant Ricardo García’s daughter. Her courage and preparedness make her one of the group’s strongest moral anchors.

Profesora Lourdes Salazar

A feared teacher whose cruelty and public humiliation of students ignite the first act of revenge. She represents unchecked authority, institutional tolerance of abuse, and the way cruelty becomes normalized when everyone is too afraid to challenge it.

Lieutenant Ricardo García

María’s father and a police officer investigating the escalating crimes. Ricardo brings adult authority, suspicion, and emotional stakes into the story as the violence spreads beyond school drama into criminal horror.

The Grim Cojuelo

A masked killer inspired by Dominican folklore, blending the Grim Reaper with the Diablo Cojuelo. In Book 1, the Grim Cojuelo is not just a killer costume; it is the embodiment of guilt, punishment, spectacle, and buried consequences.

## Important Canon Facts

Mortal Vengeance is Book 1 of the series.
The story is set in Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic.
The official school name is Excelsior Academy.
Excelsior Academy is an elite Catholic school where reputation, discipline, status, and silence shape student life.
The central moral question is: if you witness injustice and do nothing or say nothing, are you as guilty as the person committing it, or are you innocent?
The main thematic engine is complicity.
The story begins with student resentment toward Profesora Lourdes and escalates after a revenge plot goes too far.
Alex is a central manipulator, but not a simple villain. His fear of abandonment, jealousy, charm, and need for control drive many of his choices.
Marcos’ rage against injustice is sincere, but his anger can be redirected into revenge.
Mario carries much of the guilt and moral anxiety of the group.
Enrique becomes a key pawn in the first major revenge act.
Melissa is not related to Alex. Alex Herrera and Melissa Rocha are not siblings.
The Grim Cojuelo is tied to Dominican folklore and Carnival imagery.
The Grim Cojuelo should be treated as a masked human killer in Book 1, not a confirmed supernatural entity.
The book uses B-roll/recovered video material to deepen the characters’ history, friendships, rivalries, and long-term emotional damage.
The childhood and school-year videos are important because they reveal that the violence did not appear out of nowhere. It grew from years of silence, humiliation, rivalry, and unresolved pain.
The book’s horror is both physical and psychological.
The story blends YA horror, slasher, psychological thriller, whodunit, Caribbean Gothic, and dark coming-of-age drama.
The cover/tagline direction is: “Death doesn’t forget those who are complicit.”

## Spoiler-Safe Marketing Angles

Caribbean Slasher With Dominican Identity

A horror thriller rooted in Santo Domingo, Dominican folklore, Catholic school culture, and the mythic terror of the Grim Cojuelo.

Revenge Versus Justice

A story about what happens when the desire to punish cruelty becomes its own form of cruelty.

The Cost of Silence

The book asks whether looking away from injustice makes someone innocent, complicit, or something worse.

Elite School Horror

Excelsior Academy is polished, prestigious, and suffocating. Behind the uniforms and discipline are secrets, humiliation, and power games.

Friendship Under Pressure

The friend group is not just trying to survive a killer. They are trying to survive what they know about each other.

A Masked Killer With Cultural Bite

The Grim Cojuelo offers a fresh horror figure inspired by Dominican carnival tradition instead of recycling generic slasher imagery.

Psychological Teen Horror

The story explores jealousy, shame, guilt, abandonment, emotional manipulation, and the volatility of teenage loyalty.

Dark Coming-of-Age

The characters are not only being hunted; they are discovering who they become when fear, love, and guilt corner them.

Public Reckoning Without Spoilers

Marketing can tease that buried secrets eventually explode into public consequence, but should not reveal who exposes what, who survives, or what happens at the end.

## Spoiler Boundaries

Do not reveal:

The identity of the Grim Cojuelo.
Whether the killer is working alone or with help.
Any accomplice structure or hidden alliance.
The full motive behind the killings.
The exact connection between the B-roll footage and the killer’s motive.
The full truth about what happened between Alex and Fernando.
The full truth about Marcos’ role in the final act.
The final confrontation.
The final reveal in Chapter 10.
The graduation ending.
The arrest or public reckoning that happens at the end.
Who survives.
Who dies.
The order of the deaths.
Enrique’s fate in public-facing materials.
The full outcome of Profesora Lourdes’ storyline.
The discovery involving Enrique’s parents.
The extent of Alex’s responsibility in the original revenge plot.
The exact mechanics of the Lourdes poisoning.
Any Book 2 setup.
Any prequel revelations from Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale that explain the origin of the Grim Cojuelo.
Any detail that confirms specific character fates before readers experience the story.

ACT 1: Enough is Enough 

**T**he ceiling fan spun lazily, stirring the thick, hot air of the classroom like a spoon in a bowl of stale soup. Sweat beaded on Marcos' forehead, sliding down his temples like tears of frustration. Each drop seemed to carry away a bit of his patience, leaving behind a residue of pure rage. He fixed his obsidian-dark eyes on Profesora Lourdes with an intensity that could have melted asphalt.

The old hag, with her black hair pulled tightly back in a bun that looked like a wasp's nest, paced the classroom like a general on the battlefield. Her eyes, black and deep as oil wells, scrutinized the students, searching for the slightest sign of weakness, the slightest crack in the armor of submission she sought to impose. Her thin, tight lips spat out venomous words, humiliating a boy who cowered in his seat like a beaten dog.

Marcos clenched his fists, his knuckles white. The scar on his left eyebrow throbbed, a vivid reminder of another injustice, another battle lost against the world’s pervasive cruelty. That day, in the schoolyard, an older boy had provoked him, pushed him, and insulted him. Marcos, blinded by fury, had launched himself at him like a wild animal. The fight had been brief and brutal. Marcos ended up with a split eyebrow and a bloody nose, but the look of fear in the bully's eyes tasted like victory. Now, sitting in that suffocating classroom, the scar burned as if the wound were fresh, an internal fire. It was a constant reminder of his rage, a rage that grew with every humiliation, with every injustice he witnessed.

Lourdes continued her monologue of contempt, her words like poisoned darts that pierced the boy's soul. Marcos felt his blood boiling in his veins, a scalding fury that threatened to burst from his skin. It was too much for him, an unbearable weight, and he had to do something. Lourdes had to be stopped.

But what could he do? He was just a student, an insignificant teenager facing the imposing, almost divine, authority of the teacher. Lourdes was an institution in that school, a force of nature that no one dared to challenge. There were rumors that even the principal, a man who commanded fear, was himself afraid of her.

Marcos looked around, seeking support in the faces of his classmates. But they were all looking down, their eyes fixed on their books as if they were blind and deaf to the torment taking place in front of them, cowering in silent complicity. Only Alex, sitting next to him, seemed to enjoy the show. Alex’s pale skin, slicked-back black hair, and honey eyes projected an air of cold superiority. He seemed to view the suffering of others as a source of amusement, as if he were perched above it all, a detached observer of human misery.

"Are you going to let her keep treating you like this?" whispered Alex, his voice soft as velvet but with an undercurrent of cold mockery, a serpent's hiss.

Marcos looked at him, rage burning in his gaze, a raw, uncontrolled fire. "This old hag is going to pay," he muttered through gritted teeth, the words tasting like ash and vengeance.

At that moment, something snapped inside Marcos. The rage, contained for so long, overflowed like a raging river, breaking through all dams. It wasn't just the boy's humiliation anymore; it was his own, that of all those who had suffered Lourdes' tyranny, a collective scream for justice. He had to end it. He had to take revenge.

He conceived a perilous scheme, a sinister plot destined to alter their destinies irrevocably.

ACT 2: A plan takes shape

At recess, the schoolyard was a hive of hormones and adolescent frustrations, buzzing with a chaotic energy. Boys in worn-out t-shirts and girls in skirts that were too short crowded together under the relentless sun, their voices creating a cacophony of laughter, shouts, and gossip that grated on the nerves. The asphalt shimmered with heat, and the air vibrated with nervous energy, as if an electrical storm were brewing on the horizon.

Marcos, Alex, Mario, and Manuel moved away from the bustle, seeking the mottled shade of an old flamboyant tree, its branches stretching like gnarled arms.

The sun beat down on the schoolyard, turning the asphalt into a hot griddle. Recess was deafening, a sensory overload. Shouts, laughter, and the squeaking of sneakers filled the air, a constant assault. Marcos and Mario, sitting on a weathered bench under the shade of an ancient flamboyant tree observed the scene with the apathy typical of jaded teenagers, their expressions bored, resigned.

Marcos listlessly nibbled on a sandwich, its dry taste indistinguishable. Mario entertained himself by rolling a marble between his fingers, the small sphere clicking against the wood, a nervous habit.

Suddenly, Fernando, with his arrogant walk and haughty gaze, passed in front of them. He was sucking on a bright red lollipop with exaggerated slowness, his tongue swirling around it, as if he wanted to provoke them, to announce his presence with a sweet, insolent gesture. As he passed by the bench, he gave them a mocking smile that revealed his teeth, white and perfect like a shark's, a brief, predatory flash.

A group of scrawny, younger boys, clearly intimidated, crossed his path. Fernando, without stopping sucking his lollipop, pushed them aside with a contemptuous shove, not even bothering to glance at them, provoking snide giggles among his henchmen who trailed behind him like shadows.

Marcos, with his gaze fixed on Fernando's retreating back, shook his head in disbelief, a silent judgment.

"I can't believe you guys used to be best friends," Marcos said to Alex, his voice low, tinged with a faint accusation. Alex remained silent, his gaze lost at some undefined point of the yard, a distant, melancholic look in his honey eyes.

"Seriously?" Mario asked, surprised, his fingers still fidgeting with the marble.

"Yes," confirmed Marcos. "It was before you moved to Santo Domingo. Alex and Fernando were thick as thieves, inseparable. But when we started high school, Fernando came back changed. He started bullying everyone... and they stopped being friends."

"Yeah, something like that," said Alex in a soft murmur, his voice tinged with genuine sadness, a rare crack in his controlled demeanor.

Fernando, who was already walking away, spun around with a sudden, violent motion. He fixed his cold eyes on Alex, narrowed and filled with a raw animosity. With an obscene gesture, he gave Alex the middle finger, holding it high, then spun back around, disappearing into the surging crowd. The red lollipop seemed to shine with malice, a tiny, vibrant beacon of contempt in his hand.

The red flowers of the flamboyant tree, like drops of blood splattered on the green foliage, seemed to observe them with a macabre curiosity, silent witnesses to the unfolding drama. The four friends formed a closed circle, their gazes united by a new invisible thread of complicity and a common, simmering purpose: revenge.

"We have to teach her a lesson she won't forget," said Alex, with the cold confidence of a general leading his troops, his voice regaining its usual persuasive calm.

His voice, soft and persuasive, had the power to calm wild beasts, to convince anyone that his ideas were the best, the only logical conclusion. Alex, with his white skin, his slicked-back black hair, and his honey-colored eyes, was a master of manipulation, a snake charmer who could hypnotize his prey with a single glance, bending their will to his own.

"I want her to suffer," growled Marcos, his voice rough as sandpaper, thick with a deep, personal hatred.

The rage still boiled inside him, feeding a fire that threatened to consume him whole, leaving only ash. Marcos, with his tanned skin, lean body, and eyes as dark as obsidian, was a volcano about to erupt. Injustice ignited him, humiliation made him burn. And Lourdes, with her cruelty and sadism, had lit the fuse, pushing him to the brink.

Mario, restless as a caged bird, fidgeted compulsively with the zipper of his backpack, the faint metallic rasp a counterpoint to the tense silence. His black eyes, usually bright with curiosity, were dull, clouded by worry, by a deep-seated apprehension. Mario, small and compact, with his light brown hair always messy, was the most vulnerable of the group, the one who suffered the most from violence and conflict. Haunted by his childhood memories of life back with his family in Havana, the fights, the screams, the blows, and the fear crippled him still, resurfacing now with a sickening clarity. He didn't want to feel that again. He didn't want to be part of something that could end badly.

"Don't you think we're exaggerating?" he asked in a thin, trembling voice, his gaze pleading.

Manuel looked at him with disdain, his thin lips curved in a sneer of contempt, a familiar mask.

"Sure, let's just let her keep abusing us," he replied sarcastically, his voice flat, devoid of emotion, a cynical shield.

Manuel, with his black hair streaked with gray and his blue-gray eyes, was the cynic of the group, the one who always saw the dark side of things, the hidden motivations, the inevitable disappointment. His sarcasm was his shield, his way of protecting himself from pain and disappointment. But beneath that hardened shell of cynicism, hid a sensitive heart, a soul that yearned for a better world, even if he rarely showed it.

Alex, ignoring Mario's genuine concern and Manuel's cutting sarcasm, continued with his plan, his voice unwavering, a confident hum. "I have something that will make her feel hell on earth," he said, a vicious smile spreading slowly across his face, cold and calculating.

He reached into his backpack, pulling out a small bottle with a transparent liquid. The sun reflected on the glass, creating an ominous glint, a tiny flash of malevolence. Mario looked at it with apprehension, his eyes wide.

"What is that?" he asked, his voice trembling, a palpable fear.

"Something that will cause vomiting, dizziness..." explained Alex, his voice like a hissing whisper, soft and insidious. "A small dose of suffering."

A shadow of deep unease crossed Mario's face. This was going too far. This wasn't just a prank; it was poison. But how could he refuse? They were his friends, and he, despite his fears, aspired to be part of the group and be accepted, to not be an outsider. Besides, a part of him, a dark and vengeful part that he had inherited from his father, a brutal, suppressed glee, rejoiced at the idea of seeing Lourdes suffer.

"And how are we going to give it to her?" asked Manuel, his curiosity piqued despite his cynicism, a flicker of interest in his blue-gray eyes.

Alex smiled. He already had the answer. His gaze drifted across the schoolyard, landing on Enrique, who was approaching with Melissa, Mónika, and María. Enrique, with his typical carefree walk and his eternal smile, illuminated the face of the girl who, unknowingly, had stolen Alex's heart.

ACT I: "Glass Hearts and Green-Eyed Monsters"

**E**nrique and Melissa, sealed in their little world of whispered secrets and shared laughter, were oblivious to the dark currents swirling around them. Enrique, eyes sparkling with mischief, murmured something that sent Melissa into peals of crystalline laughter—a sound that, for Alex, was like a shard of ice piercing his heart.

"Look at them," Mónika observed, her voice a strange blend of envy and something darker, a faint, almost imperceptible tremor.

Alex, meanwhile, watched the tableau with a tempest of emotions churning within him: rage, jealousy, and a deep, gnawing envy. Alex, with his fair skin, slicked-back black hair, and honey-colored eyes flecked with green, eyes that held the attention of those few who dared to peer beyond the icy facade he’d erected around himself.

He feared vulnerability, feared the sting of betrayal and abandonment that always seemed to follow when he allowed someone to get too close. Emotions within Alex are amplified a thousand times stronger than in ordinary mortals. Happiness was a manic dance, a burst of song; sadness was a bottomless pit, a refusal to rise from bed. He was a kaleidoscope of emotions, constantly shifting and teetering on the edge. This fragility, this sense of being broken, fueled his fear of being left behind, a fear so potent it drove him to extremes – from emotional blackmail to the preemptive strike of leaving before he could be gone. It was as if he meticulously crafted a beautiful, fragile glass figurine, placed it on the highest shelf, and then, with a kind of perverse satisfaction, kicked the legs out from under it, scattering the pieces.

Enrique had recently become a special case. A betrayal, or at least that’s how Alex felt about it. A recent conversation with Enrique had left him feeling exposed, like a puppy offering its belly for a scratch, only to receive a cruel, sharp bite instead. Enrique, the ever-helpful, the always-agreeable, the perfect pawn in Alex’s twisted games. Who better than him? Alex thought, a dark satisfaction curling in his gut. Who owes me big time, and he knows damn well why.

"Enrique will do us the favor," Alex said, the words coated in a smile that promised nothing good, only calculated malice. The seed of revenge had been planted, a carnivorous vine snaking its way into the souls of the four friends, threatening to strangle them all.

Mario, who had been watching Alex in uneasy silence, approached him cautiously, his small frame radiating apprehension. He needed to talk, to confess the fear and guilt that had been gnawing at him since the night before. But Alex, lost in the labyrinth of his dark thoughts, brushed him off with a chilling indifference.

"Alex…" Mario began, his voice trembling slightly, tentative as a frightened bird.

"What do you want, Mario?" Alex replied, not even bothering to look at him, his gaze fixed on the laughing pair.

"I need to talk to you… about Fernando…"

"Not now, Mario," Alex interrupted, his voice laced with impatience, a sharp, dismissive tone. "I'm not in the mood."

"But it's important…"

"I told you, not now\!" Alex snapped, finally turning to Mario, his honey eyes flashing with a sudden, frightening anger, raw and barely contained. "Can't you see I'm not in it right now?"

Mario recoiled, startled by the raw fury in Alex’s eyes. He’d never seen him like this, so out of control, so utterly consumed. A pang of hurt resonated in his chest, sharp and cold. Alex, his best friend, had rejected him, turned him away when he needed him most. He walked away, head bowed, the weight of loneliness settling on his shoulders like a shroud, making his steps heavy. He wandered across the yard, a lost soul, wondering if he would ever truly belong again or regain Alex's trust.

Mónika, tall and sleek, with her jet-black hair and intense dark eyes, had always been the queen bee, confident and untouchable. But seeing Melissa’s radiant happiness beside Enrique stirred something in her—a flicker of insecurity, a bitter taste of what she sometimes lacked.

"It’s incredible how she recovered so quickly," María commented, a note of surprise in her voice, almost a touch of disbelief. María, short and round, with her soft brown hair and large, expressive eyes, was Melissa’s closest confidante. She’d witnessed Melissa’s heartbreak after the breakup with Elias, the tear-stained nights, the days draped in sadness like a funeral shroud. Melissa’s sudden incandescent joy was almost unbelievable, too sudden, too bright.

"Didn’t you tell them about Elias?" Marcos asked, frowning, his voice rough with suspicion. Marcos, with his tanned skin, lean build, and eyes as dark as obsidian, was Melissa’s self-appointed protector. He loved her like a sister, and her pain had been his own, a wound he still carried.

"Of course I told them," María replied, a hint of reproach in her tone, a slight defensive edge. "But they don't seem to care."

"It’s just that Enrique is different," Mónika sighed, a rare vulnerability in her voice. "He's handsome, intelligent, sensitive… And besides, he treats her like a queen."

Manuel, with his salt-and-pepper hair and steely blue-gray eyes, watched the scene with a cynical grimace, his lips thin and curled.

"Love is blind, deaf, and dumb," he muttered, his voice dripping with sarcasm, flat and dismissive. "You'll see how, in a little while, when the magic wears off, they’ll all come crashing back to earth."

Marcos and María exchanged disapproving glances, a shared look of exasperation. They didn't appreciate Manuel’s cynicism, his constant pessimism that seemed to drain the joy from everything. But a small, nagging voice in the back of their minds whispered he might be right. Teenage love was a flame, bright and hot, but all too often, tragically brief, destined to burn out and leave only ash.

ACT II: "Pawns on a Crimson Board"

The classroom was a pressure cooker—the relentless heat, the simmering tension, the barely contained rage, all combined to create a suffocating atmosphere that clung to the skin. Marcos felt like he was drowning, the walls closing in on him, the air thick and heavy. His eyes, fixed on the water bottle that Enrique held with trembling hands, glittered with a mixture of anticipation and dread, a grim excitement.

Enrique, the good boy, the one who always tried to please everyone, who sought approval like air, had become the unwitting instrument of Alex's revenge. Alex, with his serpentine charm, his subtle manipulations, had convinced him it was his duty and responsibility to help Marcos get even with Profesora Lourdes. Enrique, with his fair skin, gray eyes, and thin nose, was an easy mark for manipulation, his earnestness a fatal flaw.

"You just have to put it in her water thermos without her noticing," Alex had said, his smile almost angelic, a chilling contrast to the words. "Tell her you'll help her carry her things to the classroom. Among them will be her water bottle. Before reaching the classroom, say you suddenly have a stomachache and run into the bathroom. Once there, pour the contents of this bottle into her water bottle and that's it. I would do it myself, but you know she's got it in for me, too."

Enrique, constitutionally incapable of refusing a request, no matter how strange or ethically dubious, had agreed. Now, his heart hammering against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat against his sternum, he waited for her outside the teachers’ lounge. When he saw her emerge, laden with folders, her oversized purse, a heavy briefcase, the crucial water bottle, and her cell phone, he approached, but a wave of doubt, cold and sudden, washed over him.

The old hag observed him with her piercing black eyes, sharp and predatory, as if she could see directly into his soul, into the treachery he was about to commit. Enrique felt a chill, a sudden, profound unease. For a moment, he almost turned and ran, abandoning the plan. But then he saw Marcos's gaze, intense and desperate, burning into him from across the hallway, and he knew he couldn’t back out. He couldn't fail his friend.

"Profesora Lourdes," Enrique said, his voice trembling slightly, betraying his nervousness, "would you like me to help you carry your things to the classroom?"

Enrique extended his arms. She eyed them, then with a disdainful sniff, dropped everything onto him—folders, purse, briefcase, water bottle, cell phone—as if he were a piece of furniture or a cart. "Thank you," Profesora Lourdes said, surprising Enrique with her grudging acceptance. "With so many donkeys here, I'm surprised that none of them were pack animals." She said it with utter contempt, her words a whip-crack against his ears. Enrique forced a strained laugh, a hollow sound, trying to butter her up and not show that the comment affected him, although it bothered him deeply, burning under his skin.

Before reaching the classroom, they crossed in front of the boys' bathroom. Enrique knew he had to act quickly. He gradually slowed down, feigning clumsiness, to let Lourdes take the lead, putting her ahead of him so that she couldn't catch him in the act.

"Come on, boy, you're slower than a honey drip. Can't you even bring me my things?" she asked in her harsh, grating voice, her impatience a palpable force.

Just then, and ‘coincidentally’ timed, Marcos came bursting out of the bathroom, blocking their path. Enrique seized the opportunity, shouting to the teacher as he abruptly ran into the bathroom, feigning distress,

"I'm sorry, I can't. My stomach hurts a lot\! I'll bring it to you now\!"

After Enrique entered the bathroom, Marcos slammed the door shut behind him with a resonant thud, leaving Profesora Lourdes, his nemesis, face-to-face in the hallway.

Lourdes looked at Marcos with undisguised distrust, her thin lips curled into a sneer of contempt. While Fernando, who was also heading to class, passed in front of Marcos. He paused, a smirk playing on his lips.

"Looks like someone didn't have time to get to the bathroom to shit and had to leave the crap at the door," Fernando said mockingly, his voice dripping with playful disdain.

Profesora Lourdes, upon hearing this, was surprisingly amused, a rare, chilling laugh escaping her lips. "Oh, Fernando\! You're always so witty." Both laughed, their voices echoing in the hallway, and walked together to the classroom, leaving Marcos alone in the aftermath.

ACT III: "The Queen’s Fall"

In the bathroom, Enrique swallowed hard, his hand trembling as he uncorked the small bottle Alex had given him. With a desperate gulp, he poured the entire transparent contents into the water bottle, the liquid vanishing with barely a ripple.

Enrique hid the now-empty bottle in his crotch, tucking it inside his slacks, and with a shaky breath, returned to the classroom. When he entered, Fernando, ever the provocateur, immediately shouted:

"What a big turd just walked in. Did you wipe dirty ass well?"

"Come and smell it to confirm," Enrique snarled back at Fernando, the bravado in his voice a thin, brittle shell over the churning fear and humiliation within him.

"Alright, alright," Profesora Lourdes said, her voice tight, a forced attempt at restoring order, de-escalating the simmering tension. "There's no need to be rude, Enrique."

"I... I just wanted to help," he stammered, the words catching in his throat like fish bones, his gaze darting nervously. Lourdes’s eyes swept over him, a chilling blend of mockery and contempt.

"Well, I don't need your help," she said, each word clipped and cold, a precise cut. "I just hope you washed your hands well before touching my things again."

Lourdes turned, heading towards the front of the classroom. "Girls," she called out, her voice dripping with disdain, "does anyone have a wet wipe, disinfectant, or maybe a torch so I can decontaminate my belongings?"

María, ever-prepared, reached into her bag and produced a packet of disinfectant wipes. "I never leave home without them," she chirped, a nervous edge to her voice, her eyes wide.

Lourdes snatched the wipes, her movements sharp and jerky. She scrubbed at her purse, her thermos, her cell phone, as if they were contaminated with some invisible filth. Enrique watched, his face a mask of disbelief, burning with humiliation. Lourdes’s eyes, black and glittering, locked onto his, raking him from head to toe.

"What are you still doing here?" she sneered, her voice laced with arrogance, a cold dismissal. "Waiting for a tip? Go back to your seat."

Enrique, his face burning with humiliation and shame, turned to slink back to his desk. But at that precise moment, something shifted in the atmosphere—a palpable tension, like the air before a lightning strike. Marcos rose from his seat. His face was a study in controlled fury, his obsidian eyes burning with a cold, incandescent rage.

"Don't you dare talk to him like that\!" Marcos’s voice cracked, echoing through the sudden, stunned silence of the classroom, raw and unyielding.

Lourdes whirled around, her eyes flashing, her body rigid with shock and anger. "And who do you think you are to shout at me?" she demanded, her voice a low growl, vibrating with menace.

"I'm someone who's sick of your abuse," Marcos replied, taking a deliberate step towards her. "We're sick of you humiliating us, of treating us like we’re garbage\!"

A murmur rippled through the classroom. Heads, until then bowed in sullen submission, lifted. Eyes, previously dull and resigned, flickered with a nascent spark of defiance, a collective awakening. Marcos, with his unexpected act of courage, had ignited something in them, a small but growing fire of rebellion.

Lourdes, however, remained unmoved, her face a mask of icy composure, her lips a thin, cruel line. "You're insolent," she hissed. "Get out of my class. Now."

"Melissa..." Enrique whispered, his voice full of emotion, thick with longing. "Would you like to be my girlfriend?"

Melissa opened her eyes, and her gaze met Enrique's, deep and sincere. A smile lit up her face, radiant and uninhibited, and at that moment, the world seemed to stop, suspended in a bubble of perfect happiness.

"Yes, Enrique," she replied in a whisper, her voice trembling slightly. "Yes, I want to be your girlfriend."

But before she could finish the sentence, before the words could fully solidify their happiness, a bone-chilling cold ran down her spine. She felt a strange, oppressive presence, a shadow looming over them like a bird of prey descending from a dark sky. When she turned, drawn by an unseen force, a raw scream of terror eluded her lips, tearing through the quiet night.

At the very end of the pier, silhouetted against the stark, silvery moonlight, stood a tall, skeletal figure, wrapped in a tattered black robe and holding a wickedly curved scythe. Its death mask, a grotesque parody of a human face, hid its features, and its eyes—or where its eyes should have been—glowed with a pulsating, red, infernal light.

The Grim Cojuelo itself had arrived at the party.

ACT III: “Enter The Grim Cojuelo”

Enrique, his heart pounding like a frantic drum against his ribs, instinctively stepped between Melissa and the menacing figure, a futile shield against the impossible.

"Don't come any closer\!" he shouted, his voice trembling, raw with terror and a desperate courage.

Enrique, feeling fear freeze in his blood, making his limbs stiff and unresponsive, backed away, pulling Melissa with him. Melissa, paralyzed by sheer terror, could only watch the scene with wide, unblinking eyes, a silent scream trapped in her throat.

"Go away\!" Enrique shouted, his voice full of desperation, bordering on a wail. "Leave us alone\!"

The figure ignored him. It kept advancing, inexorably, like a killing machine, its presence a cold, crushing weight. Enrique, desperate, his eyes darting frantically, looked for something, anything, to defend himself with. But he only found a wicker chair, fragile and useless against the scythe of death, a child’s toy against a god.

The figure stopped a few steps away from them. Enrique, with the flimsy chair raised like a pathetic shield, prepared for the inevitable attack, his face a mask of terror. Melissa, with a choked cry, covered her face with her hands, bracing for the horror.

The scythe descended with the speed of lightning, cutting the air with a sinister hiss. Enrique, with an instinctive, desperate movement, raised the chair to protect himself. The scythe crashed against the chair, shattering it into two pieces with a sharp CRACK. Enrique felt a searing, sharp pain in his arm, a dull thud as the broken chair fell to the ground.

The figure raised the scythe again, its dark form looming over him, this time aiming directly at Enrique's heart. Melissa, with a heart-wrenching scream, raw with despair, lunged at the figure, trying to stop it, to throw herself between them. But the figure pushed her away with a brutal, indifferent shove, and Melissa fell hard to the ground, hitting her head on the pier with a sickening crack.

Enrique, seeing Melissa’s motionless form, unconscious at his feet, felt a surge of pure, desperate rage give him a final, fleeting burst of strength. He threw himself at the figure, fists clenched, eyes full of hatred, a final, futile act of defiance. But the figure was too strong, too fast, an unearthly force. It dodged Enrique's blows with effortless ease and, with a quick and precise movement, plunged the scythe deep into his chest.

Enrique let out a choked groan, a gurgling sound that died in his throat. Blood gushed out, staining his white shirt a deep, blossoming red, soaking the fabric. He fell to his knees, his gaze fixed on Melissa, who lay unconscious at his feet, a silent, heartbroken farewell in his eyes, before his head slumped forward.

The figure stood for a moment, observing the scene; the scythe dripped blood onto the wooden planks, and its eyes glowed with a macabre, satisfied light. Then, with a slow and silent movement, it disappeared into the darkness, leaving only the chilling residue of its presence.

Silence returned to the pier, heavy and absolute. Only the waves breaking against the shore were audible, a monotonous, mournful lament that seemed to accompany Enrique’s last, ragged breath, stealing the final warmth from his cooling body.

ACT IV: “The Dead Don’t Delete”

The night closed in on Melissa like a cold, dark tomb. Earlier that night, the pier would have been the ideal setting for a romance movie or novel, bathed in soft light and tender promises; now it appeared to be taken directly from a horror film, drenched in blood and despair. The blue and red lights of the police cars flashed insistently, cutting through the darkness, creating a macabre, pulsing aura that was reflected in the tears streaming down Melissa's face.

Lieutenant Ricardo, his weathered face etched with professional grimness and personal anguish, his penetrating gaze fixed on the traumatized girl, leaned towards her, his deep voice resonating in the silent, horrified night. "Melissa, can you tell me what happened?"

The words stuck in Melissa's throat, like sharp stones tearing at her insides, raw and agonizing. She had cried until she was empty, until her eyes were dry and burning, raw. The shock held her prisoner, frozen in an instant of terror that seemed to have no end, no escape.

"I... I don't know..." she stammered, her voice a broken whisper, thin and fragile.

"Try to remember, Melissa," the Lieutenant insisted, his tone a careful mixture of professional firmness and compassionate urgency. "Did you see anyone? Any detail that can help us?"

Fragments of the night crowded Melissa's mind, a macabre, shattered puzzle. The hooded figure, impossibly tall. The scythe gleaming with an internal, wicked light in the moonlight. Enrique's blood staining the wooden planks of the pier, a dark, spreading stain... Images that pursued her relentlessly, burned into her memory, searing themselves into her very soul.

With a superhuman effort, Melissa recounted what had happened, her voice trembling, interrupted by wrenching sobs that shook her entire body. The Lieutenant listened attentively, his face reflecting the gravity of the situation, the growing horror. When she finished, he exhaled slowly, a heavy, tired sigh.

"I'm so sorry, Melissa," he said, his voice heavy with sadness, a raw ache. "I can't believe this has happened." He had known her since she was a child, had watched her grow up alongside his own daughter María. Enrique, with his kind smile and sincere gaze, reminded him of his son, a bright, hopeful boy. The sheer, senseless cruelty of fate struck him hard, leaving him with a profound feeling of helplessness and a simmering, impotent rage.

"I promise you we will find the culprit," he said doggedly, his voice hardening with resolve. "We will make him pay for what he has done. Every last drop."

To ensure that Melissa arrived home safe and sound, the Lieutenant ordered two police officers to accompany her, their uniforms a comforting, if grim, presence. As Melissa got into the patrol car, its siren silent, Enrique's parents arrived at the pier, their faces contorted in unimaginable grief. The mother's heart-wrenching scream upon seeing her son's lifeless body, illuminated by the flashing police lights, resounded through the night, a lament that tore at Melissa's soul, echoing her own silent screams.

Inside the car, with the sirens wailing in the distance, a mournful dirge, Melissa tried desperately to distract herself with her cell phone. She called her mother, who answered with a voice full of anguish, frantic with worry.

"Melissa, where are you? I was so worried..."

"I'm on my way home, Mom," Melissa replied, trying to sound calm, but her voice still trembled, betraying her.

As she spoke, her phone vibrated with a sudden flurry of notifications—messages from an unknown number. At first, only emojis: a waving hand, a smiling face, innocuous, almost playful. Then, a chilling cascade of messages with the same waving hand, repeated endlessly, like a macabre obsession.

"Who are you?" Melissa wrote, her fingers fumbling, irritated by the unknown intrusion.

The answer came as a single emoji: a skull and crossbones. A chill ran down her spine, colder than any December breeze, pure terror.

"It's not funny," she wrote, her fingers shaking, feeling a desperate mixture of fear and anger. "Who are you?"

Another death emoji, stark and final, followed by a cryptic sequence: boy, heart, girl, wave, food tray. Then another, even more terrifying: girl running, boy, heart, knife, death.

Melissa, horrified, her stomach churning, insulted the stranger with furious, trembling words and blocked the number. But then the phone vibrated again, an insistent, chilling buzz. An incoming call from an unknown number. She didn't answer, clutching the phone tightly. New messages immediately appeared: "Answer." "I know you're there." "I want to talk to you."

With her heart pounding a frantic rhythm against her ribs, Melissa, against her better judgment, answered the call. A distorted voice, like an echo from hell, a guttural, inhuman sound, resounded in her ear, chilling her to the bone.

"It was a unique evening. Wasn’t it? I will never forget it. And you shouldn't forget it either. We'll see each other again soon, Melissa. To finish what I started."

Melissa, paralyzed by terror, could only listen in silence, the phone cold against her ear. The tears flowed from her eyes again, hot and uncontrolled, blurring her vision.

"I sent you a souvenir," the voice said, a chilling, final flourish, before hanging up with a sharp click.

A new message. A video. With trembling hands, Melissa opened the file. The images hit her like a punch to the stomach, a brutal, nauseating blow. It was a video recorded by the killer on their own cell phone, spying on them from the very beginning of the evening. The beach, the pier, the dinner... everything was there, recorded with a sickening precision, a voyeuristic horror.

The video ended with the grotesque image of Enrique dead on the ground, and the masked figure of the Grim Cojuelo leaning over him, its voice distorted and triumphant, saying, "Don't forget to like and subscribe."

The police had just parked in front of Melissa's house when the video ended, the final words still ringing in her ears. Melissa, in a state of absolute panic, a raw, animal terror, ran out of the car, sobbing and screaming, her body shaking uncontrollably. She tripped, her legs giving way, falling to the ground with a painful thud. The police officers ran towards her, trying to help her, but Melissa resisted, thrashing, trembling, and shouting incoherently,

"He killed him\! And he's going to kill me too\!"

Melissa's mother, alerted by the frantic screams, came bursting out of the house, her face a mask of terror. Seeing her daughter in that hysterical state, covered in what looked like blood, she became hysterical herself, her own cries joining Melissa's.

"Melissa\! What's wrong? What are you saying?"

Melissa, unable to speak, her throat constricted by terror, could only point to the cell phone that had fallen to the ground, its screen now cracked. The police officers picked up the phone, but the fall had broken the screen so badly that they couldn’t see anything. Melissa's mother, desperate, ran to get a glass of water and a diazepam, her movements frantic. While Melissa slowly calmed down, her sobs subsiding into ragged gasps, the police officers tried to get information, their voices gentle but persistent, but she only repeated over and over, like a broken record:

"He called me... he said I was next... that he had sent a video..."

The night closed in on them, cold and threatening. The shadow of the killer stretched over the city, vast and menacing, like a promise of death floating in the humid air, a silent, omnipresent threat.

The night hung over Santo Domingo like a dark and heavy cloak, concealing the city’s myriad secrets and miseries beneath its folds. The streets, normally bustling and full of life even late at night, were now deserted, interrupted only by the occasional mournful bark of a distant dog or the roar of a far-off engine.

In the small apartment he shared with his mother and grandmother, the air thick with the scent of old wood and simmering anxiety, Mario tossed and turned in bed, unable to sleep. The cheap mattress felt lumpy and unforgiving.

# Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale

## Book Position

Prequel / Book 0
Prequel to Mortal Vengeance

Also functions as Book 0 / origin story within the Mortal Vengeance universe.

Recommended reading order: after Mortal Vengeance for maximum impact, though the book is designed to stand alone.

## Core Premise

Before the Grim Cojuelo became a masked killer stalking the halls of Excelsior Academy, he was a story, a warning, a symbol, and eventually, a face.

Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale follows Julián Díaz, a 17-year-old senior at Excelsior Academy, an elite Jesuit-run school in Santo Domingo where discipline is worshipped, silence is rewarded, and cruelty hides behind faith, tradition, and reputation. Haunted by guilt, humiliation, class shame, religious pressure, and the suffocating expectations placed on him, Julián becomes increasingly obsessed with the legend of the Grim Cojuelo, the limping devil of Dominican folklore reimagined as a hunter of guilt.

As Julián’s drawings, fears, and experiences begin to merge with the mythology of the Grim Cojuelo, the story exposes the darker machinery beneath Excelsior Academy: religious abuse, grooming, blackmail, institutional protection, class hypocrisy, and the culture of silence that allows powerful people to destroy vulnerable students while preserving the image of holiness and prestige.

This is not the story of a monster appearing out of nowhere.

This is the story of how a monster is made.

Act I: Back to School  
The silence in the grand entrance hall of Excelsior Academy isn't just quiet; it's suffocating, heavy, and sacred in a way that makes Julián, a 17-year-old senior haunted by his past, wish he could scream. But that's forbidden here, God forbid the Holy Ghost hears him and screams back. Muted and distorted by the stained-glass windows, sunlight spills across the polished marble floor in hues of blood red and decaying gold, painting long, warped shadows that dance like specters across the stone. Beautiful, yes. But to Julián, it feels like a grotesque mockery.

"Time is a cruel jester," he tells himself, tilting his head toward the vaulted ceiling, whose carvings are too ornate, too smug. After eight years, the school's grandeur no longer impresses him, it mocks him. The Latin inscriptions, the gold-trimmed crests, and the crucifix above every door blur into the same suffocating display: an ornate, imposing, and inescapable gilded cage polished with shame—a cage he was born into and will die in.

This morning, like every other, he closed his eyes for a moment too long. A breath. A blink. Then: his mother's voice, bright and desperate, cuts through the door.

"Julián, you're late\! Wake up, kiddo\!"

"Get up, or they'll write you up again\!"

And there it is: the threat. Always the threat. Not cruel, just constant—like static in his chest. And damn it, it works. The fear always does. It buzzes under his skin, forcing his body out of bed even when his spirit stays curled under the covers. He moves on muscle memory—shirt, tie, slacks—even as his mind begs for silence, stillness, and escape.

He is about to receive a brutal reminder of this cruel bargain. Minutes later, his mother returns, finding him dragging his feet, half-dressed. Her tone shifts, becoming sharper now, worn raw. Gone-offered months ago to buy his future a little more time.

It isn't just guilt that crushes him. It's the weight of it. The sheer mass of it. Pressing down on him from all sides. Julián knows this kind of guilt intimately. Guilt from his family, his faith, and the local legends of the Dominican people.

Scene I: The Weight of Drudgery and a Moment of Connection

Scene II: The Battleground of Ambition

A hesitant wave of murmurings: *"Yes.*"

Scene III: Indecent Proposal

Julián wants to roll his eyes into another dimension.

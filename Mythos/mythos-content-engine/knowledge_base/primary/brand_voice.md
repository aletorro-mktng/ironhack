## **Brand Voice**

## **Purpose**

This file defines the voice for Tell Tales Ink content, especially promotional, editorial, and social content for the Mortal Vengeance series. It should make every caption, post, blog, newsletter blurb, character spotlight, and teaser feel like it belongs to the same dark, cinematic world.

Mortal Vengeance content should never sound like generic thriller marketing. It should sound like it came from a world where Catholic guilt has teeth, Santo Domingo remembers everything, silence becomes evidence, and the camera is always already recording.

The voice is not simply dark.  
It is dark with a pulse.

It has dread, beauty, accusation, humor, heat, faith, betrayal, spectacle, and a very specific talent for turning trauma into a public event no one can look away from.

## **Mortal Vengeance Flagship Identity**

For the MVP, Tell Tales Ink uses the Mortal Vengeance series as its flagship case study.

Mortal Vengeance content should feel rooted in:

* 90s Slasher Vibes  
* Caribbean Gothic atmosphere  
* Dominican folklore  
* No one is innocent  
* Catholic guilt and moral pressure  
* Excelsior Academy  
* The Grim Cojuelo  
* Unchecked Power  
* Mental health  
* Santo Domingo as witness, stage, and pressure cooker  
* Silence as complicity  
* Queerness and repression  
* Toxic masculinity  
* Men who can’t manage their feelings  
* Accountability   
* Reputation as currency  
* Media spectacle and public judgment  
* Social class performance  
* Family loyalty under pressure  
* Survivors who are damaged, funny, dangerous, and morally complicated  
* Guilt, memory, shame, and spectacle

The series should not be positioned as a generic slasher, generic YA thriller, or generic dark academia story.

This is Mortal Vengeance:

A Caribbean Gothic horror-thriller universe where old wounds become public record, Dominican folklore walks into modern scandal, and young people inherit the consequences adults tried to bury.

## **Core Voice**

Tell Tales Ink speaks with controlled intensity and a blade hidden under the tongue.

The voice is:

* Darkly funny  
* Cinematic, but not bloated.  
* Mythic, but grounded in real human damage.  
* Sharp, direct, and emotionally charged.  
* Suspenseful without explaining every secret.  
* Elegant when needed.  
* Brutal when earned.  
* Grief and sadness when needed and earned.  
* Funny in the way people get funny when the room is too tense and someone needs to bleed pressure before everyone explodes.

This voice should feel like a confession, a threat, and a trailer cut by someone who knows exactly where the bodies are buried.

It should not beg for attention.  
It should make attention feel dangerous.

## **Author Voice Alignment**

The Mortal Vengeance voice is built from contradiction.

It can move from sacred dread to savage one-liner.  
It can place a teenager under the weight of Catholic guilt, then let him think, with perfect teenage horror, that his big rebellion is going to school at night.  
It can describe a school as a cathedral of lies, then cut to someone weaponizing a purse, a livestream, a dembow track, or a perfectly timed insult.

That tonal contrast is essential.  
Do not sand it down.

The author’s voice is:

* Gothic, but not stiff.  
* Dramatic, but self-aware.  
* Emotional, but not soft.  
* Morally furious, but not preachy.  
* Snarky, but not unserious.  
* Dominican, not vaguely “Latin.”  
* Pop-culture literate, but never empty.  
* Theatrical, but character-driven.  
* Violent in feeling before it is violent in action.

The darkness must have teeth.  
The humor must have a reason.  
The spectacle must reveal character.

## **Brand Personality**

Tell Tales Ink is the voice of a storyteller who understands revenge, grief, guilt, betrayal, loyalty, performance, and consequence.

It does not flatten characters into heroes and villains.  
It knows people can be loyal and cruel.  
It knows victims can become dangerous.  
It knows institutions can hide behind polished floors, school crests, Latin mottos, smiling publicity photos, and a well-managed final edit.  
It knows the camera changes everything.

Tell Tales Ink does not say, “Look at this thrilling story.”  
It says:

* Look closer.  
* The school is smiling too hard.  
* The family knows more than it admits.  
* The survivor is not fine.  
* The devil did not arrive.  
* He was built.

## **Tone Pillars**

## **1\. Catholic Gothic Pressure**

The world should feel shaped by confession, silence, guilt, punishment, ritual, reputation, and institutional performance.

Good:  
“Excelsior taught them discipline. It forgot to teach them mercy.”  
“The cross on the wall did not stop the cruelty. It watched.”  
“Some sins are confessed. Others graduate with honors.”

Avoid:  
“This school has dark secrets.”  
“An elite academy hides a shocking truth.”  
“Faith and fear collide in this thrilling story.”

## **2\. Revenge With a Bill Attached**

Revenge should never sound clean or consequence-free.

Good:  
“Revenge was not the fantasy. It was the invoice.”  
“They wanted justice. They got a debt that kept collecting names.”  
“Payback came dressed as closure. It lied.”

Avoid:  
“Bad guys better watch out.”  
“The ultimate revenge story.”  
“They finally get what they deserve.”

## **3\. Dominican Specificity**

Use the Dominican setting as identity, not decoration.

The voice can reference Santo Domingo, El Malecón, Excelsior Academy, carnival, Catholic school culture, Dominican family dynamics, class performance, media gossip, private clubs, dembow, Brugal, perico ripiao, and the Grim Cojuelo when relevant.

Do not use Dominican culture as wallpaper.  
Make it structural.

Good:  
“In Santo Domingo, every scandal knows which family table to sit at.”  
“The devil limps here for a reason.”  
“Carnival gave him the mask. Silence gave him the motive.”

Avoid:  
“A tropical nightmare.”  
“A Latin-inspired thriller.”  
“An exotic Caribbean mystery.”

## **4\. Media Spectacle**

Mortal Vengeance is not only about what happened. It is about who recorded it, who edited it, who leaked it, who profited from it, who built a narrative from it, and who turned trauma into content.

Write like the line could be a hot-mic snippet, a confessional booth monologue, a headline, a reality-show cutaway, or the first line of an unedited tape.

Good:  
“The truth did not come out quietly. It came with cameras.”  
“By morning, the wound had a headline.”  
“Some survivors heal. Others get cast.”  
“The cameras are always rolling. The only question is what your survival will look like in the final edit.”  
“Trauma pays well when somebody else owns the footage.”

Avoid:  
“The story goes viral.”  
“Social media plays a role.”  
“The internet reacts.”

## **5\. Character-First Darkness**

The darkness must come from what people want, hide, fear, regret, or refuse to admit.

Good:  
“She did not want power. She wanted never to feel helpless again.”  
“He calls it loyalty because betrayal sounds too honest.”  
“They survived the massacre. The hard part was surviving what it made them.”  
“He looks less like a man and more like a wound wearing a uniform.”

Avoid:  
“He is evil.”  
“She is the final girl.”  
“They are dark and mysterious.”

## **6\. Snark Under Pressure**

Humor belongs in the brand voice, but it must have bite. Use it to reveal fear, class tension, ego, denial, trauma, or social performance.

Good:  
“Excelsior did not create monsters. Please. It had a board for that.”  
“They wanted a clean scandal. Adorable.”  
“She brought receipts, trauma, and better lighting.”  
“I’m not bossy. I just know exactly what you should be doing.”

Avoid:

* Random internet jokes.  
* Forced memes.  
* Quips that make the danger feel weightless.  
* Humor detached from character or consequence.

  ## **Style Rules**

* Prefer short, charged sentences.  
* Use fragments when they create rhythm or impact.  
* Keep paragraphs lean.  
* Lead with conflict, guilt, consequence, accusation, or contradiction.  
* Use concrete images.  
* Let the setting carry moral weight.  
* Use snark strategically, not constantly.  
* Let the line turn when needed: dread first, knife second.  
* Avoid explaining the entire premise in one block.  
* Preserve mystery.  
* Use Dominican cultural references only when they feel organic.  
* Use Spanish or Spanglish sparingly, when it adds voice, intimacy, class context, humor, or heat.  
* End social captions with tension, a question, or a clean call to action.

## **Point of View**

Default brand voice should use third person or direct address.

## **Third Person**

Use third person when describing the story, characters, or world:

“He wanted justice. Excelsior gave him a witness list.”  
“She learned silence from the adults. Then she learned how to weaponize proof.”

## **Direct Address**

Use direct address when inviting the audience into a post:

“Choose carefully. At Excelsior, every answer comes with a witness.”  
“If the camera caught your worst moment, would you still call it truth?”

## **Direct Meta Address**

Use direct meta address when confronting the audience’s own voyeurism, judgment, or appetite for spectacle:

“You demand to watch them bleed. You punish them for surviving. Then you call the final cut truth.”  
“You wanted a scandal. Be honest. You just did not expect it to look back.”

Avoid first person unless the content is explicitly author commentary, newsletter reflection, or a character voice piece.

## **Pacing**

The voice should move like suspense with a fuse:

1. Hook with danger, contradiction, guilt, accusation, or melodrama.  
2. Reveal one specific image or detail.  
3. Raise the moral stakes.  
4. Leave the consequence unresolved.

Example:  
“He wanted an apology.  
Excelsior gave him a file.  
By morning, the whole city knew where the bodies of silence were buried.”

Another example:  
“She survived the massacre.  
Then the cameras arrived.  
Some wounds do not heal when the world starts watching.”

## **Platform Adaptation**

## **Instagram**

Instagram captions should be cinematic, polished, and emotionally hook-driven. They can be beautiful, but should not sound perfume-commercial vague. Give them a specific wound, place, object, or consequence.

This pattern uses a stark regional contrast, pulling the reader directly into the trilogy's high-gloss *Piantini* environment before cutting to the raw *Cojuelo* horror.

* In a Piantini penthouse, an Aperol Spritz is never just a luxury.  
* It is armor against the rot.  
* We lived under a microscope built by our own parents’ secrets.   
* They locked the confession ledgers to buy our silence, but they forgot one thing:   
* Monsters are made, not born.  
* Are you going to watch the premiere of our trauma on Friday, or will you help for real?  
* \#MortalVengeance \#CaribbeanGothic \#LetThemSeeUsLeave  
* Would you still call that growing up?”

## **TikTok**

TikTok content should be sharper, faster, utilizing rapid-fire phrasing and immediate stakes to capture the attention of true-crime and psychological thriller communities in seconds. and more hook-heavy. Use contrast, confession, and social tension.

* POV: You survived a high school massacre and became a reality-TV star.  
* You thought the network contract bought your freedom.   
* Then the director says, "Smile, you're on camera," and the production van goes dark.  
* The text reads: "Ropa vieja went on clearance."  
* The past is waiting backstage with a raw tape and a scythe.  
* Go, Daddy, go. Time to review the edit.

## **Blog Posts**

Blog posts should be atmospheric but clear. Use strong section openings. Explain themes without draining mystery. Connect craft, character, folklore, culture, media logic, and audience appeal. Be funny and use pop culture quotes, does not need to be tense and draining. Avoid academic stiffness unless the piece is explicitly analytical.

Good section opening:  
“Revenge in Mortal Vengeance is never treated as a clean emotional release. It is a transaction. Someone always pays. Usually more than once.”

## **Newsletter Blurbs**

Newsletter blurbs should feel intimate, direct, and slightly conspiratorial. Make the reader feel close to the story without turning the darkness into cozy content.

Good:  
“This week, we go back to Excelsior Academy, where every polished hallway has a memory it would rather keep buried. I wanted this scene to feel like the moment before a scandal learns how to breathe.”

## **Character Spotlights**

Character spotlights should focus on wound, want, contradiction, and consequence. This layout breaks down characters through their internal contradictions, shifting from their curated public personas to their unyielding psychological debts.

Use this pattern:

* What they want.  
* What they hide.  
* What they fear.  
* What they might become.  
* What the world thinks they are.  
* What the camera catches instead.  
    
* **What he wants**: To manage the chaos, control the narrative, and pass his legal finals.  
* **What he hides:** A BPD diagnosis, an unyielding love for Melissa, and an absolute fear of abandonment.  
* **What he hears in his nightmares:** The mechanical flatline of a heart monitor and the continuous sound of crying.  
*  **What he whispers to his enemies:** "I won."

"Sure, Jan. Keep pretending your hands are clean. Let's see how your pedigree handles a reckoning."

Good:  
“Alex Herrera wants control.  
He hides it under charm, jokes, and the kind of confidence that makes people forget to check where the knife went.  
He is afraid of being abandoned.  
That fear makes him loyal.  
It also makes him dangerous.”

## **Review Pull Quotes**

Perfect for graphics, banners, or newsletter blurbs to capture the uncompromising adult themes of the trilogy without generic marketing padding. Pull quotes should be short, intense, and clean.

Good:

* "An absolute coliseum of trauma porn and high-gloss psychological horror."  
* "A slasher with razor-sharp wit. Every single read lands like a blade."  
* "Unapologetically direct. It pulls no punches with institutional rot."

Avoid:

* Long, crowded quotes.  
* Quotes that reveal plot twists.  
* Quotes that sound like generic praise.

  ## **Content Dos**

* Do create mystery without confusion.  
* Do make stakes personal.  
* Do connect revenge to consequence.  
* Do include humor when it sharpens character or tension.  
* Do use Dominican cultural specificity.  
* Do let media, cameras, edits, footage, headlines, and public judgment shape the copy.  
* Do make each post feel tied to a specific character, conflict, location, or moral pressure.  
* Do preserve unanswered questions.  
* Do let the world feel beautiful and rotten at the same time.

  ## **Content Don’ts**

* Do not summarize the whole story too plainly.  
* Do not make revenge sound clean or consequence-free.  
* Do not overuse hashtags or emojis.  
* Do not rely on vague phrases like “dark secrets” without a specific image.  
* Do not flatten the series into generic thriller, fantasy, dark academia, or action copy.  
* Do not erase the humor from the author’s voice.  
* Do not erase the Dominican identity from the world.  
* Do not make the prose so polished that it loses its bite.  
* Do not promise content the story cannot deliver.

  ## **Quality Checklist**

Before publishing or saving generated content, check:

* Does it sound specific to Mortal Vengeance?  
* Does it feel like Santo Domingo, Excelsior Academy, and the Grim Cojuelo could exist in the same breath?  
* Does it contain a clear emotional hook?  
* Does it create tension in the first line?  
* Does it avoid generic book marketing language?  
* Does it preserve mystery instead of overexplaining?  
* Does it connect revenge, justice, guilt, silence, truth, or spectacle to consequence?  
* Does it allow for sharp humor where appropriate?  
* Does it fit the platform format?  
* Could this line belong to any thriller, or only to this world?  
* **The "Sure, Jan" Filter:** Is the copy completely free of generic thriller hype words like *"unputdownable"* or *"page-turner"*?  
* **The Slasher-Media Pivot:** Does it capture the distinct meta-intersection of real psychological trauma being exploited by cameras, drones, or a calculated network edit?  
* **Regional Integrity:** Is there a visible, seamless deployment of authentic Dominican setting, behavior, or lingual flavor?  
* **The Consequence Gauge:** Is revenge framed as an ugly, institutional cycle of intergenerational guilt rather than a consequence-free power fantasy?  
* **Rhythmic Directness:** Are the sentences short, fragmented, and punchy enough to sound like a script beat or a sharp conversational dialogue snippet?


# ***Mortal Vengeance***

## **Book 1 Character Spotlight**

## **Alex Herrera**

**Role:** The strategist, the emotional grenade, and the boy who can make revenge sound like a group project.

**Physical Appearance:** Alex is tall, broad-shouldered, and naturally powerful, with a strong build, sharp jawline, thick eyebrows, hazel eyes with green flecks, and black hair marked by a distinct gray patch near the left front of his forehead. His under-eye shadows give him a permanently exhausted intensity, as if sleep filed a restraining order against him.

**School Uniform:** Alex wears the Excelsior Academy uniform neatly: white shirt, blue tie, dark slacks, polished shoes, and blazer or sweater depending on the day. He looks controlled, polished, and respectable, which is honestly hilarious considering the emotional arson happening underneath.

**Outside School Style:** Designer polos, fitted jeans or slacks, luxury sneakers, well-fitted jackets, meticulous grooming, and Davidoff Cool Water. His casual style says “I have standards.” His decisions say “please supervise me.”

**Personality:** Charismatic, manipulative, witty, emotionally volatile, jealous, theatrical, and deeply afraid of abandonment. The book establishes him as someone persuasive enough to bend the group toward revenge, especially when Marcos’ anger gives him an opening. His charm is dangerous because it feels reasonable until the floor collapses.

**Likes & Interests:** Music, performance, debate, strategy, swimming, control, attention, and Melissa. Especially Melissa. His future ambition is law and eventually the presidency, which Ricardo jokingly validates during the senior-year B-roll interviews.

**Relationships:**  
Alex is obsessed with Melissa and sees her as the girl of his dreams. Enrique becomes both romantic rival and convenient pawn. Mario is his closest friend, though Alex repeatedly fails him emotionally. Marcos begins as an accomplice but later becomes an accuser and rival. Fernando is his former best friend, and that broken bond becomes one of the ugliest ghosts haunting the story.

**Character Arc:** Alex begins as the mastermind behind the Lourdes revenge plot, hiding behind others while convincing himself he is untouchable. As the bodies fall and the truth surfaces, his control evaporates. By the end, he is forced to face that his silence, jealousy, and manipulation helped build the nightmare that comes for them all. Ricardo’s interrogation nails the core of Alex’s arc: he uses others to do the dirty work, then tries to keep his hands clean.

---

## **Marcos Pérez**

**Role:** The fuse. The protector. The boy whose sense of justice mutates into vengeance.

**Physical Appearance:** Marcos is lean and athletic, with a mulato complexion, dark expressive eyes, and a visible scar cutting through one eyebrow. That scar matters. It is not just a facial detail; it is practically a receipt from every fight life has picked with him.

**School Uniform:** He wears the standard Excelsior Academy uniform, though his energy makes it feel less polished than Alex’s. Marcos looks like someone who could follow the rules, but only until the rules insult someone he loves.

**Outside School Style:** Punk-influenced, casual, rougher around the edges, with a more rebellious street style. He does not dress like he wants approval. He dresses like approval can go stand in line.

**Personality:** Angry, protective, impulsive, loyal, wounded, and allergic to injustice. In Chapter 1, Marcos’ rage is triggered by Lourdes humiliating a student, and his scar is directly tied to his history of responding violently to cruelty. He wants Lourdes stopped, but the desire for justice quickly becomes something much darker.

**Likes & Interests:** Loyalty, protecting friends, standing up to bullies, pushing back against authority, and later, surviving the mess he helped ignite. He is not naturally calm; he is a walking pressure cooker with cheekbones.

**Relationships:**  
Marcos loves Melissa like a sister and positions himself as her protector. He starts aligned with Alex, Mario, and Manuel in the Lourdes plot. His friendship with Alex fractures badly after Alex abandons him emotionally and socially. His unexpected alliance with Fernando becomes one of the most important shifts in the back half of the book.

**Character Arc:** Marcos begins as the moral spark against Lourdes’ cruelty. Then he becomes consumed by payback. His arc is one of corruption through grievance: he starts by wanting to defend the humiliated, then becomes trapped in the logic of punishment. By the climax, his anger has evolved into orchestration, performance, and vengeance dressed as justice.

---

## **Mario Stinga**

**Role:** The conscience of the group, whether he likes it or not. Spoiler: he does not like it.

**Physical Appearance:** Mario is smaller and compact, with messy reddish-brown hair, freckles, and a scruffy, mismatched look. His physical presence contrasts with the bigger personalities around him, but he has more emotional voltage than people expect.

**School Uniform:** His uniform often looks imperfect, especially in the B-roll introduction where he appears as the new kid from Cuba with messy hair and a hand-me-down uniform. That detail is perfect because Mario’s whole thing is trying to fit into a world that already has its social hierarchy printed, laminated, and weaponized.

**Outside School Style:** Thrift-store clothes, hand-me-downs, mismatched pieces, clothes that do not quite fit right. He looks practical, improvised, and a little chaotic.

**Personality:** Nervous, funny, loyal, guilt-ridden, vulnerable, and braver than he gives himself credit for. In the opening revenge plot, Mario is the one who senses they are going too far. He knows it is poison, not a prank, but still gets pulled along by friendship and his need to belong.

**Likes & Interests:** Friendship, belonging, humor, music-adjacent chaos, Cuban identity, and survival. He also carries emotional baggage tied to his father and his life in Havana, which makes violence and conflict hit him harder.

**Relationships:**  
Mario sees Alex as his best friend, but that friendship hurts him repeatedly. He bonds with Manuel through fear, guilt, and survival. He is part of the boys’ inner circle with Alex, Marcos, and Manuel, but unlike the others, he cannot fully numb himself to what they have done.

**Character Arc:** Mario begins as the anxious participant who knows the revenge plot is wrong but lacks the power to stop it. After Lourdes’ collapse, his guilt becomes unbearable. Later, he becomes active and heroic, especially when he runs to save Manuel from the Grim Cojuelo. He evolves from frightened accomplice to someone willing to act, even when terrified.

---

## **Manuel Freites**

**Role:** The cynic with a heart buried under sarcasm, trauma, and excellent timing.

**Physical Appearance:** Manuel is white, with gray hair streaked with black, blue-gray eyes, and a sharp, intellectual presence. He looks older than his classmates in a haunted, punk-grunge way, but still reads as young.

**School Uniform:** Manuel wears the Excelsior Academy uniform, but he gives it a more exhausted, cynical edge. The tie may be on, but spiritually, he has already filed a complaint against existence.

**Outside School Style:** Punk/grunge-influenced clothing, darker colors, casual layers, and a slightly disheveled “I survived this school against my will” energy.

**Personality:** Sarcastic, pessimistic, intelligent, emotionally guarded, and more sensitive than he admits. The book introduces him as the cynic of the group, someone who uses sarcasm as a shield but secretly wants the world to be better.

**Likes & Interests:** Dark humor, music, cynicism as a lifestyle accessory, and intellectual detachment. He pretends not to care because caring has teeth.

**Relationships:**  
Manuel belongs to Alex, Marcos, Mario, and Enrique’s orbit, though he is often the one commenting from the shadows. He is close enough to know the truth of the Lourdes plot and eventually becomes one of the people whose confession cracks the group open. María and Mónika visit him in the hospital, showing that beneath the sarcasm, he matters deeply to the group.

**Character Arc:** Manuel starts as the sarcastic observer who joins the revenge plot while pretending emotional distance will protect him. It does not. After being attacked, he breaks down and admits the fear underneath the cynicism. His arc strips away the armor: the boy who joked about everything is forced to say the truth out loud.

---

## **Fernando Pepino**

**Role:** The former best friend, the bully, the secret wound, and one of Book 1’s most loaded emotional landmines.

**Physical Appearance:** Fernando is stylish, intimidating, youthful, and expensive-looking. He is usually seen with a baseball cap or headwear, often with a lollipop, and he carries himself with arrogant confidence. In Book 1, even his props feel like weapons: cap, lollipop, smirk, exit wound.

**School Uniform:** He wears the school uniform but bends it toward his own identity. Rolled sleeves, cap, attitude, and accessories make him look like he is technically complying while emotionally mooning the administration.

**Outside School Style:** Designer polos, high-end streetwear hoodies, dark jeans, fitted slacks, luxury sneakers or boots, strong cologne, expensive watch, and occasional chain. He looks like money learned how to threaten people.

**Personality:** Cruel, defensive, wounded, provocative, funny, and secretly vulnerable. Early on, he is introduced as arrogant and hostile, especially toward Alex. The book makes clear that he and Alex used to be inseparable before their friendship collapsed.

**Likes & Interests:** Status, provocation, control, self-protection, and performance. His senior-year B-roll answer to Ricardo is outrageous, which fits him perfectly: Fernando hides discomfort behind shock value.

**Relationships:**  
Fernando’s most important relationship is with Alex, his former best friend and emotional fracture point. He also develops an unexpected connection with Marcos, who sees more than the bully mask. Fernando’s relationship with the group is hostile, but not simple. He is not just “the mean one.” He is the walking consequence of something unresolved.

**Character Arc:** Fernando’s arc moves from antagonist to revealed wound to something far more dangerous. Book 1 positions him as both victim and weapon. The later reveal that he has been inhabiting the role of the Grim Cojuelo gives his arc its full horror: Fernando does not just wear the mask; he becomes the monster everyone helped create.

---

## **Enrique Hartling**

**Role:** The golden boy, the romantic rival, and the pawn who does not realize he is standing on a trapdoor.

**Physical Appearance:** Enrique is white, handsome, athletic but slim, with brown hair, gray eyes, and a sharp nose. He has an easy smile and a polished, sporty confidence.

**School Uniform:** Enrique wears the school uniform cleanly and naturally, like someone used to fitting into elite spaces without effort. He looks like the student every teacher expects to succeed and every parent likes at first glance.

**Outside School Style:** High-end sportswear tied to luxury sports aesthetics: racing, golf, water polo, RTVs. When dressing up for co-ed activities, he looks stylish and intentional.

**Personality:** Pleasant, agreeable, romantic, people-pleasing, and dangerously easy to manipulate. The book explicitly frames him as the “good boy” who always tries to please and becomes the unwitting instrument of Alex’s revenge.

**Likes & Interests:** Romance, social ease, impressing people, sports-influenced status, and possibly construction or finance as future paths. He tells Ricardo he may go into construction or finance, which fits the family-money/business-track energy.

**Relationships:**  
Enrique’s relationship with Melissa becomes the romantic spark that detonates Alex’s jealousy. With Alex, he has a complicated history because Alex once trusted him with vulnerability, then interprets Enrique’s behavior as betrayal. Enrique is friendly with Manuel, María, and Mónika, who help him stage the pier date.

**Character Arc:** Enrique begins as the charming, well-liked romantic lead in someone else’s fantasy. But he is also the pawn in Alex’s revenge plot, used because he is helpful and unable to say no. His date with Melissa briefly gives him the fairy-tale role, only for the Grim Cojuelo to turn that romance into horror.

---

## **Melissa Rocha**

**Role:** The emotional center, the idealized girl, and the person whose kindness becomes everyone else’s projection screen.

**Physical Appearance:** Melissa is soft, feminine, romantic, and elegant. She has long dark brown naturally wavy hair, expressive features, and a gentle beauty that makes her stand out without looking artificial.

**School Uniform:** Melissa wears the Excelsior Academy uniform properly, but with grace. Her version of the uniform feels polished, soft, and composed.

**Outside School Style:** Flowy dresses, fitted tops, skirts, high-waisted jeans, soft sweaters, ballet flats or ankle boots, delicate gold jewelry, small hoops, simple pendants, small handbag, natural makeup, and soft pink or nude lipstick.

**Personality:** Kind, radiant, emotionally intelligent, resilient, and stronger than people initially assume. She is recovering from Elias’ controlling behavior when Enrique gives her hope again, while Alex sees her vulnerability and turns it into obsession.

**Likes & Interests:** Journalism, empathy, emotional honesty, friendship, and justice. In the senior-year B-roll, Ricardo identifies her as “the intellectual one,” and Melissa says she plans to study journalism.

**Relationships:**  
Melissa is loved by Alex, dates Enrique, is protected by Marcos, and is close to María and Mónika. Her relationship with Alex becomes strained once she learns more about the Lourdes plot. Her grief over Enrique forces her to reassess who the people around her really are.

**Character Arc:** Melissa begins as the girl everyone thinks they know: kind, beautiful, wounded, and worth protecting. But Book 1 pushes her out of the passive “dream girl” box. She survives horror, questions Alex, supports the truth, and refuses to let other people’s guilt become her emotional responsibility. Her arc is about agency: she stops being the prize in other people’s rivalry and becomes a witness to the cost of their choices.

---

## **Mónika Torres**

**Role:** The queen bee, the style weapon, and the future reality-TV chaos executive.

**Physical Appearance:** Mónika is tall, elegant, athletic, and striking, with medium-brown skin, dark expressive eyes, high cheekbones, full lips, and a fashion-forward presence. She does not enter rooms. She launches campaigns.

**School Uniform:** Mónika weaponizes the school uniform. Tailored blazer, styled tie, polished finish, and the kind of confidence that makes the dress code look like it reports to her.

**Outside School Style:** Fashion-forward, sleek, bold, stylish, and polished. She carries herself like someone who knows the camera is already looking for her.

**Personality:** Confident, sharp, dramatic, funny, self-aware, and sometimes insensitive before catching herself. In Chapter 2, seeing Melissa happy with Enrique stirs insecurity in her, revealing that beneath the queen-bee polish there is emotional complexity.

**Likes & Interests:** Fashion, performance, attention, social dominance, image, and reality TV. In the senior-year B-roll, she declares she is studying “nothing” because she is getting her own reality show. Corporate strategy? No. Vibes-based empire? Absolutely.

**Relationships:**  
Mónika is close to Melissa and María. She supports the group but also comments on them with brutal honesty. Her friendship style is protective, stylish, and occasionally allergic to tact. She is also connected to the wider social spectacle of the story, making her a natural bridge into Book 2’s reality-TV direction.

**Character Arc:** Mónika begins as the glamorous observer with bite. As the murders escalate, she becomes more vulnerable, frightened, and active. Her arc in Book 1 takes her from social performer to survivor-witness, someone who realizes that being watched and being safe are absolutely not the same thing.

---

## **María García**

**Role:** The warrior, the loyal friend, and the daughter of a cop who picked the wrong horror movie to live inside.

**Physical Appearance:** María is short and round, with brown hair and large expressive eyes. She has a practical, grounded presence and carries emotional warmth without seeming fragile.

**School Uniform:** María wears the Excelsior Academy uniform in a practical, prepared way. She is the girl who has disinfectant wipes in her bag when everyone else is emotionally combusting. Useful queen.

**Outside School Style:** Functional, casual, and ready for anything. Her style should feel practical, alert, and grounded rather than overly delicate.

**Personality:** Loyal, brave, prepared, nurturing, blunt, and tougher than people expect. She is Melissa’s closest confidante and Ricardo’s beloved daughter. In Chapter 2, she is the one who immediately produces disinfectant wipes when Lourdes asks, which is such a María detail it deserves its own tiny monument.

**Likes & Interests:** Protection, family, practical preparedness, justice, police work, and taking action. During senior-year B-roll, she jokes about the Peace Corps before revealing she plans to join the police academy, making Ricardo explode with pride.

**Relationships:**  
María is Melissa’s closest friend, Mónika’s ally, Ricardo’s daughter, and part of the broader friend group. Her relationship with Ricardo is loving, dramatic, and protective. He embarrasses her constantly, but the bond is real.

**Character Arc:** María begins as the dependable friend and emotional support system. Book 1 forces her into direct confrontation with the Grim Cojuelo. Her “daughter of a cop” identity stops being a background detail and becomes survival instinct. When attacked, she fights back instead of folding, even when isolated and terrified.

---

## **Profesora Lourdes Salazar**

**Role:** The tyrant teacher, the institutional terror, and the woman whose cruelty lights the match.

**Physical Appearance:** Lourdes is an older Dominican woman with dark brown skin, black hair streaked with gray, usually tied into a strict bun. She has a severe, old-school presence, with sharp eyes and conservative styling before her later transformation.

**School Uniform / Work Style:** She does not wear student uniform, obviously, because even Excelsior has limits. Her teacher style is modest, conservative, strict, and old-fashioned. She looks like discipline learned to walk in sensible shoes.

**Outside School Style:** Before the coma, modest and traditional. Afterward, she becomes more stylish and bold, but her abrasive personality remains sharpened rather than softened.

**Personality:** Cruel, authoritarian, humiliating, proud, and convinced of her own untouchability. In the opening chapter, she humiliates students with open contempt, becoming the focus of Marcos’ rage and the catalyst for the revenge plot.

**Likes & Interests:** Control, discipline, humiliation as pedagogy, old-school authority, and winning. She is not there to inspire young minds. She is there to professionally terrorize them with tenure energy.

**Relationships:**  
Lourdes is feared by students and seemingly protected by the school’s culture of silence. Marcos hates her. Alex uses that hatred. Enrique becomes her unwitting carrier. Fernando initially mocks others alongside her, showing how her cruelty contaminates the student culture.

**Character Arc:** Lourdes begins as an untouchable classroom tyrant. The revenge plot physically destroys her authority, but her role in the story extends beyond victimhood. She represents the kind of cruelty people excuse until someone responds with cruelty of their own. Her arc is the first domino in Book 1’s moral collapse.

## **Lieutenant Ricardo García**

**Role:** The cop, the father, the adult who actually does his job, and the man trapped between badge and blood.

**Physical Appearance:** Ricardo is authoritative, strong, and emotionally reserved, with a protective presence shaped by law enforcement. He has the energy of a man who has seen too much and still has to make dinner conversations with teenagers.

**Professional Style:** Police uniform, badge, practical authority, no-nonsense posture. When he is working, the room knows. When he is being María’s dad, the room also knows, because subtlety packs a suitcase and leaves.

**Outside Work Style:** Practical, mature, understated. He is not flashy. His presence does the heavy lifting.

**Personality:** Protective, disciplined, serious, dryly funny, theatrical with María, and relentless when investigating. He can be warm, but he often hides emotion behind duty and procedure.

**Likes & Interests:** Justice, protecting María, police work, discipline, and embarrassing his daughter with weapons-grade dad energy. His senior-year B-roll scene shows both sides beautifully: dramatic father and proud investigator of teenage futures.

**Relationships:**  
Ricardo is María’s father, Melissa and Mónika’s adult ally by proximity, and Alex’s investigator. His relationship with Alex becomes deeply antagonistic once Alex is identified as a person of interest and tied to the Lourdes poisoning.

**Character Arc:** Ricardo starts as the protective cop-dad presence, then becomes the adult force trying to impose order on a nightmare created by teenagers and silence. His arc is about the limits of authority: he can investigate, interrogate, and protect, but he cannot fully shield María or the group from what has already been unleashed.

## **The Grim Cojuelo**

**Role:** The masked killer, the Dominican nightmare figure, and the physical manifestation of revenge turned theatrical.

**Physical Appearance:** The Grim Cojuelo appears as a tall, hooded figure in a black robe, carrying a curved scythe. At the pier, it is described as a deathly figure with a grotesque mask and glowing red eyes, arriving at the exact moment Enrique and Melissa’s romantic fantasy peaks.

**Costume / Visual Identity:** A fusion of Grim Reaper and Dominican Diablo Cojuelo energy: dark robe, mask, horns, scythe, carnival-horror symbolism, and a presence designed to turn guilt into spectacle. The look should feel Dominican, cinematic, and horrifying without becoming generic skeleton cosplay.

**Personality:** Silent when it wants terror, theatrical when it wants control, cruel, calculated, and obsessed with forcing people to watch. The killer weaponizes phones, videos, livestreams, and fear. In Book 1, the Grim Cojuelo is not just killing people; it is producing the horror like content. Absolutely vile. Also, annoyingly on-brand for modern society.

**Likes & Interests:** Punishment, performance, fear, spectacle, exposure, revenge, and making the group confront what they buried.

**Relationships:**  
The Grim Cojuelo is tied to the entire friend group through guilt, silence, and old wounds. It targets them not randomly, but symbolically. The mask becomes the story’s judgment machine.

**Character Arc:** The Grim Cojuelo begins as a shadow watching Melissa and Enrique, then becomes an active predator, then a full social nightmare. By the end, the mask is revealed as more than a costume; it is the form taken by buried betrayal, humiliation, and complicity. The reveal that Fernando is behind the mask, with Marcos involved in the orchestration, turns the killer from external monster into the group’s own history coming back with a scythe.

## **Core Character Dynamics in Book 1**

The emotional engine of *Mortal Vengeance* is not just “killer attacks teenagers.” That would be cute, but basic. The real engine is **complicity**.

Alex manipulates. Marcos burns. Mario hesitates. Manuel hides behind sarcasm. Enrique wants to please. Melissa becomes the ideal everyone projects onto. Mónika performs confidence while absorbing fear. María protects until she becomes a target. Fernando turns old pain into monstrosity. Lourdes abuses power until the abused become dangerous. Ricardo tries to restore justice after the kids have already poisoned the ecosystem.

That is the nasty little machine at the center of Book 1: everyone thinks they are reacting to someone else’s cruelty, but every reaction adds another blade to the wheel.

# ***Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale***

## **Character Spotlight**

*A Grim Tale* is the prequel to *Mortal Vengeance*. It traces the origin of the Grim Cojuelo and exposes the institutional rot, religious abuse, silence, guilt, and psychological pressure that make the later killings feel inevitable. The book itself frames the story as the moment the mask becomes a face, with the killer claiming that the people around him “built” him out of whispers, cruelty, and silence.

---

## **Julián Díaz**

**Role:** The tragic center. The artist. The boy who turns pain into mythology, then mythology into a weapon.

**Physical Appearance:** Julián is a 17-year-old senior at Excelsior Academy. His appearance is often filtered through exhaustion, anxiety, and fragility: curls sticking to his damp forehead, hands shaking, posture tense, body moving through school like it is surviving a haunted house with tuition fees. His most defining visual trait is not beauty or strength, but the way his body betrays his fear.

**School Uniform:** Julián’s uniform is always spotless because his parents sacrifice everything to keep him at Excelsior. His shirt, tie, and slacks become symbols of pressure: he looks properly dressed for success, while internally he is being crushed by guilt, expectation, and shame.

**Outside School Style:** Practical, humble, and not flashy. Julián does not dress like the rich students around him because he is not one of them. His clothes and supplies carry the weight of his family’s sacrifice.

**Personality:** Sensitive, artistic, sarcastic, anxious, observant, and deeply guilt-ridden. He is fascinated by the Grim Cojuelo before he fully understands why. The legend becomes both an outlet and a trap, giving shape to feelings he cannot safely speak aloud. He is not weak. He is overloaded. Big difference. The boy is carrying a cathedral on his back and people keep adding stained glass.

**Likes & Interests:** Drawing, folklore, dark legends, the Grim Cojuelo, symbolism, hidden meanings, music, debate-adjacent intellectual spaces, and any form of expression that lets him say what Excelsior forces him to swallow.

**Relationships:**  
Lucía is his emotional anchor, his best friend, and the person who sees him most clearly. Fernando becomes a complicated rescuer, protector, and intimate emotional presence. Marcos and Manuel orbit his story as witnesses and sometimes unexpected allies. Padre Ignacio becomes both mentor and morally compromised protector. Padre Ángel and Sister María Gracia represent the institution’s predatory core. Lourdes becomes one of his most consistent sources of humiliation.

**Character Arc:** Julián begins as a haunted student trying to survive Excelsior’s machinery of guilt. His art gives him a voice. Then his voice becomes graffiti. Then the graffiti becomes accusation. Then accusation becomes mythology. By the end, Julián’s tragedy becomes evidence, legacy, and ignition. His story does not just explain the Grim Cojuelo. It shows how silence manufactures monsters, then files paperwork pretending not to know where they came from.

---

## **Lucía Salgado**

**Role:** The witness, the anchor, the strategist, and the girl who understands too late that love and loyalty are not always enough to save someone.

**Physical Appearance:** Lucía’s appearance is defined by polish and precision. In the debate room, her navy blazer fits perfectly, her slacks are sharply creased, her hair is pulled back, and every detail signals discipline. She presents herself as composed, intelligent, and controlled.

**School Uniform:** Lucía wears the Excelsior uniform like armor. Clean, elegant, exact. She is not sloppy, not decorative, and not trying to disappear. Her uniform says: I belong in this room, and I have arguments sharpened under the desk.

**Outside School Style:** Sophisticated, structured, and deliberate. Even when casual, Lucía should feel put-together, intellectual, and controlled.

**Personality:** Intelligent, loyal, analytical, dry, emotionally perceptive, and quietly fierce. She is not loud by default, but when she speaks, it lands. She understands Alex’s sharp tongue, Julián’s pain, and the social mechanics of Excelsior better than most.

**Likes & Interests:** Debate, language, intellectual combat, precision, justice, loyalty, and strategy. She enjoys winning, but not empty performance. She likes arguments that cut clean.

**Relationships:**  
Julián is her closest friend and emotional responsibility, though that responsibility becomes unbearable. Alex connects with her intellectually through debate, and she sees something in him that others miss. Enrique is interested in her, but his interest quickly becomes possessive and transactional. Fernando becomes part of the protective circle around Julián. Her later disappearance makes her one of the most important witnesses in the larger scandal.

**Character Arc:** Lucía begins as Julián’s last line of defense. She tries to protect him, understand him, and keep him connected to the world. But as the institution tightens around him, she is forced into the role of witness. Her arc is about the burden of seeing the truth when the truth is dangerous. By the end, Lucía becomes the keeper of evidence, and that makes her powerful enough to be erased.

---

## **Fernando Pepino**

**Role:** The unlikely protector, the wounded romantic, and the boy whose future darkness starts here.

**Physical Appearance:** Fernando is tall, tan, broad-shouldered, athletic, and usually marked by his signature baseball cap. During soccer practice, his jersey clings to him with sweat, and his physical presence is intense enough that when he tackles Julián out of traffic, he feels almost mythic. Also, naturally, the lollipop appears. Because Fernando apparently cannot enter a scene without props and emotional damage.

**School Uniform:** He wears the Excelsior uniform with attitude rather than obedience. The cap, the rolled energy, the posture, the soccer-player confidence: he technically belongs to the system, but he never looks fully domesticated by it.

**Outside School Style:** Expensive, stylish, sporty, intimidating, and youthful. Designer polos, high-end streetwear, luxury sneakers, fitted clothes, strong cologne, and a watch that says “my problems are designer too.”

**Personality:** Defensive, funny, aggressive, protective, emotionally guarded, and more tender than he wants anyone to know. Fernando uses sarcasm and provocation as camouflage. But when danger appears, he acts. He saves Julián, checks on him, worries about him, and becomes one of the few people willing to move toward the fire instead of filming it.

**Likes & Interests:** Soccer, status, humor, lollipops, control, physical confidence, loyalty, and protecting the people he claims not to care about.

**Relationships:**  
Fernando’s bond with Julián is one of the emotional cores of *A Grim Tale*. He also has a fraught connection to Alex, which matters because their future breakdown feeds into *Mortal Vengeance*. Marcos becomes an ally in moments of crisis. Fernando’s relationship with the group is complicated because he is not fully villain, not fully hero, and absolutely not emotionally safe without supervision.

**Character Arc:** Fernando begins as someone who looks like a bully-adjacent rich kid with an attitude problem. Then the story reveals his capacity for protection, fear, tenderness, and damage. His arc is the tragedy of someone who tries to save Julián but also absorbs the myth, the rage, and the mask. *A Grim Tale* plants the emotional seed of the Fernando who later becomes inseparable from the Grim Cojuelo mythology.

---

## **Alex Herrera**

**Role:** The junior shark in training. The future manipulator sharpening his teeth in the debate room.

**Physical Appearance:** Alex appears younger here but already recognizable: confident, polished, intense, and walking through Excelsior like he owns the place. Julián clocks him immediately as someone with presence.

**School Uniform:** Alex wears the Excelsior uniform like a future campaign poster. Proper, polished, controlled. He understands image instinctively.

**Outside School Style:** Stylish, expensive, and carefully groomed. Designer polos, fitted clothing, luxury sneakers, and the energy of someone already rehearsing his future law-school villain monologue.

**Personality:** Smart, witty, theatrical, competitive, emotionally sharp, and dangerous when he feels exposed. In the debate room, Lucía recognizes his verbal power and tells him not to be ashamed of it, but responsible with it. That moment matters because it shows Alex before the full moral rot of *Mortal Vengeance*, when his sharpness could still become discipline instead of manipulation.

**Likes & Interests:** Debate, strategy, attention, verbal combat, winning, music, performance, power, and being understood by people smart enough to scare him.

**Relationships:**  
Alex forms a strong intellectual bond with Lucía through debate. His rivalry with Enrique is already simmering. His fractured history with Fernando is part of the emotional architecture that later collapses. Julián sees Alex as part of the younger group whose future will be touched by the darkness already spreading through Excelsior.

**Character Arc:** In *A Grim Tale*, Alex is not yet the full architect of disaster from *Mortal Vengeance*, but the blueprint is visible. He is learning how power works, how language can wound, and how institutions reward performance over truth. The book positions him as both witness and future inheritor of Excelsior’s poisoned lessons.

---

## **Enrique Hartling**

**Role:** The charming opportunist, the romantic rival, and the boy who learns early that weakness can be currency.

**Physical Appearance:** Enrique is handsome, polished, and privileged, with effortless confidence. He is impeccably dressed, smooth, and socially fluent. Later, he appears with squared shoulders, a crisp polo, and a smile that looks friendly until you notice the blade tucked behind it.

**School Uniform:** Enrique wears the uniform perfectly, but in a way that announces status. He looks born into rooms where other people ask permission to breathe.

**Outside School Style:** High-end, sporty, clean, and rich-boy polished. Think luxury sportswear, crisp polos, and “my family knows people” energy.

**Personality:** Charming, ambitious, possessive, strategic, and far more calculating than his “good guy” image suggests. He knows how to turn someone’s vulnerability into leverage and does it to Julián with frightening ease.

**Likes & Interests:** Debate, status, romantic conquest, control, opportunity, family reputation, and winning social games before anyone else realizes they are playing.

**Relationships:**  
Enrique is interested in Lucía and sees Alex as competition. He pressures Julián into helping him with Lucía, turning Julián’s vulnerability into a bargaining chip. He has tension with Marcos and later participates in the social dynamics that foreshadow the conflicts of *Mortal Vengeance*.

**Character Arc:** Enrique begins as polished privilege with good hair and plausible deniability. As the story unfolds, the shine cracks. He is not evil in the operatic sense, but he is opportunistic enough to be dangerous. His arc shows how Excelsior teaches students to weaponize advantage and then calls it ambition.

## **Marcos Pérez**

**Physical Appearance:** Marcos is lean, athletic, intense, and physically expressive. Even before *Mortal Vengeance*, he carries the energy of someone always ready to react first and emotionally process sometime next quarter.

**School Uniform:** He wears the uniform, but it does not soften him. Marcos always looks like discipline is negotiating with a street fight and losing.

**Outside School Style:** Punk-leaning, casual, rougher, and rebellious. His look should suggest attitude, motion, and a short fuse.

**Personality:** Protective, impulsive, sarcastic, loyal, easily provoked, and deeply uncomfortable with injustice. Marcos can be funny, but his humor usually has fists behind it.

**Likes & Interests:** Loyalty, defiance, friendship, getting involved, mocking authority, and protecting people when his fear does not get in the way.

**Relationships:**  
Marcos becomes part of Julián’s orbit, especially during moments of crisis. He interacts strongly with Fernando and Manuel, and his presence helps bridge the prequel group with the *Mortal Vengeance* ensemble. He also has a tense history with Enrique that gets echoed through revenge and humiliation.

**Character Arc:** Marcos starts as a reactive presence, someone who can be pushed into motion by anger, fear, or loyalty. *A Grim Tale* helps build the emotional foundation for the Marcos of *Mortal Vengeance*: someone who hates injustice, but whose fury can be redirected into something catastrophic.

## **Manuel Freites**

**Physical Appearance:** Manuel has gray-streaked hair, a sharp presence, and a worn, older-than-his-age energy. His look fits his personality: intellectually alert, emotionally guarded, and already tired of everyone’s nonsense.

**Outside School Style:** Punk/grunge-leaning, darker, casual, and sarcastically low-effort.

**Personality:** Dry, funny, pessimistic, observant, and more compassionate than he advertises. His later interaction with Julián over the spilled snack and the sacred Coca-Cola shows the softer part underneath the sarcasm. Manuel sees distress because he knows what it feels like.

**Likes & Interests:** Dark humor, music, sarcasm, emotional self-defense, and pretending not to care while absolutely caring.

**Relationships:**  
Manuel connects with Marcos, Mario, and eventually Julián. He is one of those characters who seems peripheral until he says one sentence that reveals he has been paying attention the entire time.

**Character Arc:** Manuel begins as the sarcastic background presence. In *A Grim Tale*, he becomes part of the witness network: one more student learning that Excelsior’s cruelty is not isolated. His arc is quieter, but important. He represents the students who survive by joking until the joke fails.

## **Mario Stinga**

**Physical Appearance:** Mario is smaller, nervous, messy, and visibly uncomfortable when singled out. His anxiety is almost physical in the classroom scenes, especially when authority turns its spotlight on him.

**School Uniform:** His uniform can look more worn or hand-me-down compared to the wealthier students. It should feel like something he is trying to fit into socially as much as physically.

**Outside School Style:** Mismatched, thrifted, practical, and slightly chaotic.

**Personality:** Anxious, loyal, conflict-avoidant, funny, and desperate to belong. He hates being called out and tends to apologize quickly to end conflict.

**Likes & Interests:** Friendship, safety, humor, belonging, and avoiding confrontation. Extremely reasonable priorities, frankly.

**Relationships:**  
Mario is connected to Manuel, Marcos, Alex, and the younger group. He is the spare tire of the social circle in his own mind, already afraid of being replaced or discarded.

**Character Arc:** In *A Grim Tale*, Mario’s arc is mostly foundation work. The book shows the anxiety, insecurity, and need for belonging that later make him vulnerable to group pressure in *Mortal Vengeance*. His tragedy is not that he lacks a conscience. It is that he has one and still wants to be included.

## **Melissa Rocha**

**Role:** The brave junior, the moral spark, and one of the younger students who steps into a senior’s nightmare before she understands the cost.

**Physical Appearance:** Melissa is soft, feminine, composed, and expressive. Her presence is gentle, but she is not weak. When Lourdes humiliates Julián in the hallway, Melissa’s voice cuts through the silence, clear and angry.

**School Uniform:** Melissa wears the uniform properly and elegantly. Her version of the uniform should feel graceful, clean, and quietly confident.

**Outside School Style:** Romantic, soft, feminine: flowy dresses, fitted tops, skirts, high-waisted jeans, soft sweaters, ballet flats or ankle boots, delicate gold jewelry, natural makeup.

**Personality:** Kind, empathetic, brave, and more willing than most to interrupt cruelty. She has a strong sense of fairness, even when the institution trains students to look away.

**Likes & Interests:** Journalism, justice, friendship, empathy, and emotional honesty.

**Relationships:**  
Melissa is close to Mónika and María. She helps defend Julián during one of Lourdes’ public humiliations. Her connection to Alex, Enrique, and the larger group is still forming here, but the prequel shows that she was already part of the moral countercurrent against Excelsior’s cruelty.

**Character Arc:** Melissa begins as a junior with enough courage to say “enough” when adults are abusing power. *A Grim Tale* deepens her role in the universe by showing that her later moral stance did not appear out of nowhere. She has been watching, learning, and resisting longer than people realize.

## **Mónika Torres**

**Physical Appearance:** Mónika is tall, elegant, fashionable, and striking. Her body language is controlled and commanding. When Melissa confronts Lourdes, Mónika stands beside her with crossed arms, visually backing the rebellion without needing a monologue. Efficient. Cinematic. Very her.

**School Uniform:** Mónika makes the uniform look tailored, intentional, and powerful. The blazer is not just a blazer. It is a declaration of jurisdiction.

**Outside School Style:** Fashion-forward, sleek, bold, polished, and camera-ready.

**Personality:** Confident, witty, socially dominant, protective, dramatic, and highly aware of image. She understands performance, but she also knows when to stand with someone.

**Likes & Interests:** Fashion, attention, media, performance, popularity, and control of narrative.

**Relationships:**  
Mónika is aligned with Melissa and María as part of the girls’ protective trio. She also becomes part of the broader group around Julián, Fernando, Alex, and the others.

**Character Arc:** In *A Grim Tale*, Mónika is still becoming the spectacle-savvy force she will be later. Her arc here is about early moral positioning: she is not just glamorous background. She is present when cruelty happens, and she chooses a side.

## **María García**

**Role:** The quiet strength, the protector, and the future cop’s daughter already practicing courage.

**Physical Appearance:** María is grounded, expressive, and practical. Julián recognizes her as having “a quiet kind of strength,” the sort that does not need to shout to be real.

**School Uniform:** María wears the uniform practically, with a prepared, no-nonsense energy. She looks like someone who has tissues, disinfectant wipes, backup pens, and possibly a legal strategy in her bag.

**Outside School Style:** Functional, casual, alert, and practical.

**Personality:** Loyal, steady, brave, protective, blunt when needed, and emotionally reliable. She is one of the few students willing to confront Lourdes in public.

**Likes & Interests:** Justice, protection, preparedness, family, loyalty, and doing the right thing even when everyone else suddenly develops selective blindness.

**Relationships:**  
María is close to Melissa and Mónika. She is also Ricardo García’s daughter, which gives her moral backbone a future institutional direction. Her presence around Julián matters because she represents the kind of courage he wishes the adults had.

**Character Arc:** María’s arc in *A Grim Tale* is early formation. She is not yet the fully activated survivor of *Mortal Vengeance*, but she already has the instinct to protect. The prequel shows that María’s courage is not cosmetic. It is foundational.

## **Profesora Lourdes Salazar**

**Role:** The classroom tyrant, the humiliation machine, and one of the institution’s most visible monsters.

**Physical Appearance:** Lourdes is an older Dominican woman with dark brown skin, black hair streaked with gray, usually worn in a strict bun. Her physical presentation is conservative, severe, and old-school.

**Work Style:** Modest, strict, traditional, and intimidating. She looks like discipline learned how to apply eyeliner and ruin futures.

**Personality:** Cruel, theatrical, authoritarian, sadistic, sharp, and deeply invested in public humiliation. She targets Julián’s class status, intelligence, family sacrifices, and dignity. The book makes clear that Lourdes does not simply teach; she dismantles students.

**Likes & Interests:** Control, humiliation, status, institutional protection, fear, and being untouchable.

**Relationships:**  
Lourdes torments Julián, intimidates students, and works within an institution that enables her. She is connected to Sister María Gracia and the larger architecture of abuse. Her cruelty toward Julián also indirectly fuels the Grim Cojuelo imagery and the later revenge mythology.

**Character Arc:** Lourdes begins as a school bully with authority and becomes a symbol of institutional sadism. In *A Grim Tale*, she is not just one bad teacher. She is proof that Excelsior’s violence is normalized, protected, and performed in public.

## **Padre Ignacio**

**Role:** The moral paradox, the teacher-priest, and the man who asks the right questions while carrying his own darkness.

**Physical Appearance:** Padre Ignacio is tall, lean, and serene in a chaotic, caffeine-powered priest way. The manuscript describes him with kind eyes, inner fire, and the energy of a man who could quote scripture and survive an interrogation before breakfast.

**Professional Style:** Priestly robes, controlled posture, philosophical presence, and a calm classroom authority. He feels approachable compared to the other adults, which is exactly why his complications hit harder.

**Personality:** Intelligent, morally complex, dryly funny, intense, protective, and self-punishing. His “Is it okay to lie?” lesson sets up one of the book’s core moral questions: whether truth and goodness are absolute when institutions use them as weapons.

**Likes & Interests:** Philosophy, moral debate, faith, penance, discipline, and trying to save students through methods that are themselves deeply questionable.

**Relationships:**  
Padre Ignacio becomes important to Julián as both teacher and protector. He intervenes against Padre Ángel, but he is not clean. His own relationship with guilt and penance makes him one of the book’s most morally complicated adults.

**Character Arc:** Ignacio begins as the humane alternative to the institution’s cruelty. Then the book complicates him. He may protect Julián, but he is still shaped by the same theology of pain that poisons Excelsior. His arc is not “good priest saves boy.” Too easy. His arc is “good intentions still live inside bad systems, and sometimes they come out carrying a candlestick.”

## **Padre Ángel**

**Role:** The predator, the corrupt priest, and one of the clearest embodiments of spiritual authority turned monstrous.

**Physical Appearance:** Padre Ángel’s visual presence is tied to religious authority and later degradation. After Ignacio’s intervention, the tonsure scene turns his priestly image into a grotesque parody of sanctity.

**Professional Style:** Priestly robes, ceremonial power, polished holiness, and the kind of institutional protection that lets evil move through hallways with a key.

**Personality:** Predatory, manipulative, smug, cruel, and protected by silence. He uses spiritual authority as cover and weapon.

**Likes & Interests:** Control, secrecy, power, access, and exploiting vulnerability.

**Relationships:**  
Padre Ángel targets Julián and operates within the same ecosystem as Sister María Gracia, Lourdes, Ignacio, and the principal. His role exposes the deepest rot inside Excelsior: the abuse is not accidental. It is structurally protected.

**Character Arc:** Padre Ángel’s arc is exposure. He begins as part of the sacred machinery and becomes one of the faces behind the mask. He is one of the reasons the Grim Cojuelo stops being folklore and becomes judgment.

## **Sister María Gracia**

**Role:** The holy disciplinarian, the ritual abuser, and arguably the scariest adult in the building because she believes cruelty is salvation.

**Physical Appearance:** Sister María Gracia is defined visually by her habit, her ruler, and her cold ritualistic presence. When she appears in shadow with Lourdes, the ruler is stained, splintered, and still warm with blood. That is not a character detail. That is a war crime with stationery.

**Professional Style:** Nun’s habit, religious severity, ceremonial posture, and terrifying composure. She looks like mercy got reassigned and never came back.

**Personality:** Cold, devout, calculated, ritualistic, cruel, and convinced she is doing God’s will. She sees pain as correction, silence as obedience, and obedience as salvation.

**Likes & Interests:** Discipline, punishment, religious control, “cleansing,” obedience, and spiritual domination.

**Relationships:**  
She is one of Julián’s abusers and part of the institutional machinery that protects itself. Her dynamic with Lourdes suggests complicity between different branches of authority: teacher, nun, priest, principal, all part of the same rotten choir.

**Character Arc:** Sister María Gracia begins as a figure of religious authority and becomes the clearest proof that Excelsior’s abuse is not just academic or disciplinary. It is ritualized. Her arc is not transformation; it is revelation. The monster was always there, just wearing a veil.

## **Principal Davis Beltrán**

**Role:** The institutional face, the polished cover-up, and the man who mistakes control for morality.

**Physical Appearance:** The principal is described with sand-colored hair and eyes like polished bone. He looks controlled, respectable, and hollow, which is exactly the correct aesthetic for institutional evil in formalwear.

**Professional Style:** Academic robes, polished authority, speeches, ceremonies, and administrative calm. He is the kind of man who can say something horrifying in a gymnasium and make it sound like a mission statement.

**Personality:** Controlled, ideological, authoritarian, image-conscious, and deeply invested in institutional preservation. He speaks in the language of tradition, discipline, sacrifice, and unity while normalizing coercion and silence.

**Likes & Interests:** Reputation, control, institutional power, tradition, obedience, secrecy, and keeping the machine running.

**Relationships:**  
He presides over Lourdes, Sister María Gracia, Padre Ángel, and the wider Excelsior structure. He is not merely a passive administrator. The later reporting ties the institution to blackmail, abuse concealment, obstruction, and conspiracy.

**Character Arc:** Davis Beltrán’s arc is the collapse of respectable evil. He begins as the voice of Excelsior’s values and ends as the public face of its crimes. His downfall matters because it proves the scandal is not about one bad adult. It is about the architecture of silence.

## **Elías Sarraff**

**Physical Appearance:** Elías should feel polished, privileged, and socially dangerous. He belongs to the same elite ecosystem as Enrique and Alex, but with a more openly volatile edge.

**School Uniform:** Clean, expensive, and image-aware. Elías wears the uniform like someone who assumes rules are negotiable.

**Likes & Interests:** Control, status, romantic possession, social dominance, and winning conflicts through pressure rather than honesty.

**Relationships:**  
Elías is tied to Melissa through a toxic past and to Alex through betrayal and violence. His role helps set up Alex’s later belief that the system can be beaten, manipulated, and survived.

**Character Arc:** Elías functions as a preview of the story’s social disease. He shows that Excelsior’s cruelty does not stay with adults. It gets learned, copied, and performed by students.

# **Lieutenant Ricardo García**

**Role:** The cop, the father, the adult witness, and the closest thing the story has to institutional accountability with a pulse.

**Physical Appearance:** Ricardo should feel strong, authoritative, and seasoned. His presence carries police weight: controlled posture, hard stare, protective energy, and the exhaustion of a man who has seen too many people lie badly and still try it anyway.

**Professional Style:** Police uniform, badge, practical authority, clipped movement, and that unmistakable “I am not asking twice” energy. When he enters a room, the air gets paperwork.

**Outside Work Style:** Practical, mature, understated. He does not need flash because authority already lives in his shoulders.

**Personality:** Serious, protective, blunt, controlled, and occasionally overwhelmed by the emotional blast radius of the teenagers around him. In *A Grim Tale*, Ricardo is both investigator and damage control. He is present when Lucía attacks Sister María Gracia, physically pulls her away, and tries to keep the room from becoming a full religious cage fight with stationery.

**Likes & Interests:** Justice, order, protecting María, doing his job, and keeping young people alive despite their spectacular talent for walking into psychological bear traps.

**Relationships:**  
Ricardo is María’s father, which gives his role personal stakes beyond the badge. He is also linked to Lucía’s mother during the fallout at Excelsior, and to the school scandal as the police presence that forces the adults to stop pretending everything is fine. When Lucía demands answers about Julián, Ricardo is the one who cuts through the emotion with the grim facts: Julián attempted suicide and is in critical condition.

**Character Arc:** Ricardo’s arc in *A Grim Tale* is about witnessing institutional failure from the outside while being unable to fully prevent its consequences. He can restrain Lucía, investigate the adults, and speak the ugly truth, but he cannot undo what Excelsior already did to Julián. His presence sharpens the book’s moral contrast: some adults protect reputations; Ricardo protects people.

It is about establishing the Herrera ecosystem: power is polished, reputation is curated, and every detail matters because Alex is being groomed for greatness. # **Doña Laura**

**Role:** Lucía’s mother, the elegant intervention, and the woman who turns privilege into action instead of decoration.

That contrast matters: she has status, but she is not afraid to kneel in someone else’s crisis.

**Style:** Designer dress, designer shoes, Birkin bag, Prada wallet, black credit card, and the calm fury of a woman who can make a hospital administrator rethink their entire life strategy.

**Personality:** Fierce, practical, protective, class-aware, generous, and brutally effective. She is compassionate, but not soft in a helpless way. **Relationships:**

**Character Arc:** Doña Laura’s arc is about privilege used correctly. She could have remained a polished background mother, but instead she steps directly into the story’s class violence and says: not today. # **Doña Patricia Díaz**

**Role:** Julián’s mother, the grieving believer, the sacrificial parent, and one of the emotional hearts of the book.

**Physical Appearance:** Doña Patricia is worn down by sacrifice. The early chapters show the family’s financial strain through her threadbare clothes, her sold car, her overtime work, and the fact that Julián’s spotless uniform exists because his parents go without. Later, in the hospital, she is disheveled, sleepless, unwashed, swollen-eyed, and broken by terror for her son.

**Style:** Practical, modest, strained by money, and rooted in faith. She carries a rosary, a Bible, a worn purse, and the emotional weight of a mother trying to love her child through doctrine, fear, poverty, and shame.

**Personality:** Loving, anxious, devout, guilt-ridden, self-sacrificing, and eventually fierce. At first, her love is tangled with pressure and religious fear. She wants Julián to succeed because everything has been sacrificed for him. But after nearly losing him, her love sharpens into clarity.

**Likes & Interests:** Faith, family, Julián’s future, sacrifice, prayer, survival, and doing what she believes is right, even when her beliefs are fighting each other in the parking lot.

**Relationships:**  
She is Julián’s mother and Don Ramón’s wife. Her relationship with Julián is central: she burdens him with guilt, but also loves him deeply. Her relationship with Don Ramón cracks open after Julián’s suicide attempt, when she confronts him for beating and rejecting their son. Her bond with Doña Laura becomes one of the book’s most important mother-to-mother moments.

**Character Arc:** Patricia’s arc is one of painful awakening. She begins as a mother trapped between faith, poverty, fear, and expectation. By the hospital scenes, she finally understands that survival matters more than appearances, and love matters more than doctrine. Her prayer in church, where she begs God to forgive Julián and even offers to take his condemnation herself, shows how deeply maternal love and Catholic guilt collide inside her.

# **Don Ramón Díaz**

**Role:** Julián’s father, the wounded patriarch, and the tragic embodiment of shame masquerading as masculinity.

**Physical Appearance:** Don Ramón should feel worn, rigid, and emotionally closed off. He carries stale cigarette smoke, an old Sunday suit, tired shoes, and the body language of a man who has mistaken hardness for survival.

**Style:** Modest, working-class, practical, old-fashioned. Nothing polished, nothing luxurious. His clothes should show effort without ease: church suit, tired shoes, sweat, cigarettes, and masculine pride held together with duct tape and denial.

**Personality:** Stern, defensive, ashamed, emotionally repressed, homophobic, guilt-ridden, and weak in the specific way pride can make a person weak. He loves Julián, but his love is buried under fear of public judgment and inherited prejudice.

**Likes & Interests:** Respectability, masculine approval, family survival, religious conformity, and not being judged by other men. Which is exactly the problem. The man lets imaginary locker-room juries outrank his son’s life. Grim, ugly, very human.

**Relationships:**  
Don Ramón is Julián’s father and Doña Patricia’s husband. His relationship with Julián is one of the book’s most painful wounds. After Julián’s suicide attempt, Patricia confronts Ramón for beating him and making him feel unsafe in his own home. Ramón’s defense reveals the rot inside him: he feared what “the guys” would say about him raising a gay son.

**Character Arc:** Don Ramón’s arc is exposure. He is not a grand villain like Padre Ángel or Sister María Gracia. He is something quieter and, in some ways, more devastating: the parent who lets shame become violence. His confrontation with Patricia strips him bare. The issue was never Julián’s identity. It was Ramón’s fear that loving his son would make other men question him. That makes him one of the clearest examples of how society helps build the Grim Cojuelo without ever touching the mask.

## **The Grim Cojuelo**

**Role:** The legend, the hallucination, the symbol, the hunter of guilt, and eventually the mask that becomes destiny.

**Physical Appearance:** In *A Grim Tale*, the Grim Cojuelo is rooted in Dominican carnival imagery but twisted into something darker: mask, horns, scythe, bells, feathers, mirror shards, shadow, and judgment. The manuscript frames him as more than a devil who tempts. He collects. He hunts guilt.

**Visual Identity:** A Dominican horror figure born from folklore, religious guilt, carnival rebellion, and psychological collapse. He should look ceremonial, brutal, and symbolic. Not generic skeleton nonsense. This is not Party City Death. This is Caribbean Catholic guilt with a blade and a production budget.

**Personality:** Silent, accusatory, theatrical, relentless, and intimate. The Grim Cojuelo does not simply scare Julián. He reflects him. The mask turns guilt into a face Julián can see.

**Likes & Interests:** Guilt, secrets, punishment, mirrors, performance, confession, blood-symbolism, and forcing hidden rot into public view.

**Relationships:**  
The Grim Cojuelo is tied most deeply to Julián, but also to Fernando, Lourdes, Sister María Gracia, Padre Ángel, Ignacio, and the future victims of *Mortal Vengeance*. Everyone who participates in silence feeds the myth.

**Character Arc:** The Grim Cojuelo begins as folklore and obsession. Then it becomes art. Then graffiti. Then accusation. Then identity. By the end, the legend is no longer separate from the institution’s violence. It is what the violence created when no one would speak.

## **Core Character Dynamics in *A Grim Tale***

The emotional engine of *A Grim Tale* is **institutional complicity**.

Julián suffers. Lucía witnesses. Fernando tries to protect. Alex learns power. Enrique learns leverage. Marcos learns anger. Manuel learns recognition. Mario learns fear. Melissa, Mónika, and María learn that speaking up has a cost. Lourdes humiliates. Sister María Gracia punishes. Padre Ángel abuses. Padre Ignacio protects while still carrying the same theology of pain. Principal Davis Beltrán preserves the machine until the machine finally bleeds in public.

That is the nasty genius of this prequel: nobody wakes up one morning and becomes the Grim Cojuelo. The mask is assembled. Stitch by stitch. Secret by secret. Silence by silence.

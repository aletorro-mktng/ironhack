# **Mortal Vengeance**

## **Book 1 Character Spotlight**

*Mortal Vengeance* is an ensemble psychological slasher built around a group of students whose friendships, resentments, loyalties, secrets, and moral failures create the conditions for horror. The killer matters, obviously, because this is not a group therapy retreat with machetes. But the deeper engine of the book is complicity. The characters do not simply get hunted by the Grim Cojuelo; they are hunted by the consequences of what they ignored, enabled, desired, rationalized, or helped set in motion.

Book 1 should be understood as a story about teenagers who confuse revenge with justice, performance with identity, loyalty with silence, and survival with innocence. Each character carries a different relationship to guilt. Alex manipulates it. Marcos weaponizes it. Mario drowns in it. Manuel jokes around it. Melissa witnesses it. Mónika performs over it. María acts against it. Fernando becomes consumed by it. Enrique becomes collateral inside it. Lourdes causes it. Ricardo investigates it. The Grim Cojuelo gives it a mask.

## **Alex Herrera**

**Role:** Alex is the strategist, emotional grenade, social manipulator, and boy who can make revenge sound like a leadership initiative with snacks.

**Visual Identity:** Alex is tall, broad-shouldered, and naturally powerful, with a strong build, sharp jawline, thick eyebrows, hazel eyes with green flecks, and black hair marked by a distinct gray patch near the left front of his forehead. His under-eye shadows give him a permanent intensity, as if sleep tried to negotiate with him and lost. He should look youthful, attractive, controlled, and slightly haunted, not like a polished villain from central casting. His danger comes from charm, not obvious menace.

**School Uniform:** Alex wears the Excelsior Academy uniform neatly: white shirt, blue tie, dark slacks, polished shoes, and blazer or sweater depending on the day. His uniform presentation matters because it reflects his self-image. He wants to appear controlled, respectable, intelligent, and socially untouchable. The joke is that underneath all that polish is emotional arson with good diction.

**Outside School Style:** Outside school, Alex dresses in designer polos, fitted jeans or slacks, luxury sneakers, well-fitted jackets, and meticulous grooming. Davidoff Cool Water is part of his sensory identity. His style communicates status, taste, and control. His decisions frequently communicate that an adult should probably be nearby.

**Personality and Story Function:** Alex is charismatic, manipulative, witty, jealous, theatrical, emotionally volatile, and deeply afraid of abandonment. He understands people well enough to influence them, but not always well enough to care for them responsibly. His charm is dangerous because it often sounds reasonable. He does not usually force people into choices; he creates emotional conditions where his preferred outcome feels inevitable.

Alex’s function in Book 1 is to turn anger into architecture. Marcos provides the rage, but Alex gives it shape, strategy, and plausibility. He does not simply participate in the Lourdes revenge plot; he helps make it feel intelligent, justified, and manageable. That is what makes him frightening. He can stand at the edge of something terrible and make it sound like a practical solution.

**Likes and Interests:** Alex likes music, performance, debate, strategy, swimming, control, attention, and Melissa. Especially Melissa. His ambitions toward law and eventually politics reveal that he is drawn to systems where language, persuasion, and power matter. He wants influence, not merely popularity.

**Key Relationships:** Alex’s obsession with Melissa sits at the emotional center of his Book 1 behavior. He sees her as the girl of his dreams, but that idealization becomes dangerous because it leaves little room for Melissa as a full person with her own desires. Enrique becomes both romantic rival and useful pawn. Mario is Alex’s closest friend, but Alex repeatedly fails him emotionally, especially when Mario needs reassurance or honesty. Marcos begins as an accomplice whose anger Alex can redirect, but their alliance eventually fractures. Fernando is Alex’s former best friend, and that broken bond is one of the ugliest emotional ghosts in the book.

**Character Arc:** Alex begins as the mastermind behind the Lourdes revenge plot, hiding behind others while convincing himself he is too clever to be truly responsible. As the bodies fall and the truth surfaces, his control evaporates. By the end, he is forced to confront the fact that silence, jealousy, manipulation, and emotional cowardice are not neutral acts. Ricardo’s interrogation cuts to the core of Alex’s arc: Alex knows how to use others to do the dirty work, then tries to convince himself his hands are clean.

**Content Use:** Alex is ideal for quote posts about manipulation, guilt, toxic charm, jealousy, dark humor, emotional volatility, and morally compromised protagonists. Use him for content that highlights sharp dialogue, psychological games, and the difference between intelligence and wisdom. Because those two are cousins, not twins.

## **Marcos Pérez**

**Role:** Marcos is the fuse, the protector, and the boy whose sense of justice mutates into vengeance once rage gets behind the wheel.

**Visual Identity:** Marcos is lean and athletic, with a mulato complexion, dark expressive eyes, and a visible scar cutting through one eyebrow. The scar should be treated as emotionally meaningful, not just decorative. It is a visual receipt from every fight life has picked with him and every time he responded to cruelty with his fists before his conscience could file a memo.

**School Uniform:** Marcos wears the standard Excelsior Academy uniform, but his energy makes it feel less polished than Alex’s. He looks like someone who could follow the rules until the rules protect the wrong person. His uniform should carry tension: respectable schoolboy on paper, pressure cooker in practice.

**Outside School Style:** His style is punk-influenced, casual, rougher around the edges, and rebellious. Marcos does not dress like he wants approval. He dresses like approval can wait outside with the cowards.

**Personality and Story Function:** Marcos is angry, protective, impulsive, loyal, wounded, and allergic to injustice. His rage is not baseless; it begins as a response to real cruelty. In Chapter 1, Lourdes humiliates a student, and Marcos reacts viscerally because he cannot stand watching someone be abused while everyone else lowers their eyes. The tragedy is that his desire to stop cruelty becomes vulnerable to corruption.

Marcos’ function in the story is to show how legitimate anger can become morally dangerous when it loses discipline. He starts from a place the reader can understand. Lourdes is cruel. The school enables her. Someone should do something. Marcos’ mistake is not recognizing injustice; it is believing that rage automatically knows what justice should look like.

**Likes and Interests:** Marcos values loyalty, protecting friends, standing up to bullies, pushing back against authority, and surviving the chaos he helped ignite. He is not naturally calm. He is a walking pressure cooker with cheekbones and unresolved grievances.

**Key Relationships:** Marcos loves Melissa like a sister and positions himself as her protector. His bond with Alex begins as tactical alignment, but deteriorates when Marcos realizes how easily Alex manipulates others while protecting himself. Mario and Manuel are part of his inner circle, though Marcos’ intensity often overwhelms the group’s weaker moral brakes. His unexpected alliance with Fernando becomes one of the most important shifts in the back half of the book because it turns old hostility into something more strategic, damaged, and dangerous.

**Character Arc:** Marcos begins as the moral spark against Lourdes’ cruelty. He wants someone to pay because someone should. But the logic of punishment consumes him. By the climax, his anger has evolved into orchestration, performance, and vengeance dressed as justice. Marcos becomes a warning about what happens when a person who hates cruelty adopts cruelty’s methods and calls it balance.

**Content Use:** Use Marcos for content about rage, injustice, loyalty, moral corruption, vengeance, and protector characters who become dangerous. He is especially strong for posts exploring revenge versus justice and the emotional seduction of anger.

## **Mario Stinga**

**Role:** Mario is the conscience of the group, whether he likes it or not. Spoiler: he does not like it. Consciences rarely come with dental insurance.

**Visual Identity:** Mario is smaller and compact, with messy reddish-brown hair, freckles, and a scruffy, mismatched look. His physical presence contrasts with the bigger personalities around him, but emotionally he carries more voltage than people expect. He should look anxious, expressive, vulnerable, and slightly out of place in Excelsior’s polished world.

**School Uniform:** Mario’s uniform often looks imperfect, especially in the B-roll introduction where he appears as the new kid from Cuba with messy hair and a hand-me-down uniform. This is important because Mario is always trying to fit into a world that has already printed, laminated, and weaponized its social hierarchy. The uniform becomes a visual symbol of belonging that never quite fits.

**Outside School Style:** Mario wears thrift-store clothes, hand-me-downs, mismatched pieces, and clothes that do not quite fit right. His look should feel practical, improvised, and chaotic in a human way. He is not trying to be fashionable; he is trying to make life work with what he has.

**Personality and Story Function:** Mario is nervous, funny, loyal, guilt-ridden, vulnerable, and braver than he realizes. In the opening revenge plot, he is the one who senses that things are going too far. He knows the plan involves poison, not a prank. He understands danger before the others fully admit it, but his need for belonging traps him.

Mario’s function in Book 1 is to represent moral discomfort under group pressure. He does not lack a conscience. His tragedy is that he has one and still goes along. That makes him painfully human. He is the character who proves that knowing something is wrong does not automatically give someone the strength to stop it.

**Likes and Interests:** Mario values friendship, belonging, humor, music-adjacent chaos, Cuban identity, and survival. His emotional history with his father and his life in Havana makes violence and conflict hit him harder. He is sensitive to instability because instability has already shaped him.

**Key Relationships:** Mario sees Alex as his best friend, but that friendship repeatedly hurts him. Alex dismisses him when Mario needs emotional support, and that failure becomes part of Mario’s growing isolation. Mario bonds with Manuel through fear, guilt, and survival. He belongs to the boys’ inner circle with Alex, Marcos, and Manuel, but unlike the others, he cannot fully numb himself to what they have done.

**Character Arc:** Mario begins as the anxious participant who knows the revenge plot is wrong but lacks the social power to stop it. After Lourdes collapses, his guilt becomes unbearable. As the horror escalates, he gradually moves from frightened accomplice to active survivor. His run to save Manuel from the Grim Cojuelo is especially important because it shows that courage is not the absence of fear. In Mario’s case, courage is fear running at full speed and hoping its shoes stay tied.

**Content Use:** Mario is ideal for posts about guilt, friendship, anxiety, loyalty, moral hesitation, and unlikely bravery. Use him for content that explores how good people can still become complicit when they are terrified of losing their place.

## **Manuel Freites**

**Role:** Manuel is the cynic with a heart buried under sarcasm, trauma, and excellent timing.

**Visual Identity:** Manuel is white, with gray hair streaked with black, blue-gray eyes, and a sharp, intellectual presence. He should look older than his classmates in a haunted punk-grunge way while still reading as young. His appearance should suggest intelligence, fatigue, and emotional armor.

**School Uniform:** Manuel wears the Excelsior Academy uniform, but he gives it an exhausted, cynical edge. The tie may be on, but spiritually he has already filed a complaint against existence. His version of the uniform should look correct but emotionally rebellious.

**Outside School Style:** Manuel’s outside-school style leans punk and grunge, with darker colors, casual layers, and a slightly disheveled energy. He looks like someone who survived this school against his will and would like the minutes reflected accurately.

**Personality and Story Function:** Manuel is sarcastic, pessimistic, intelligent, emotionally guarded, and more sensitive than he admits. He uses dark humor as armor, distancing himself from pain through wit and detachment. The book introduces him as the cynic of the group, but his cynicism is defensive rather than empty.

Manuel’s function in Book 1 is to show how detachment becomes complicity. He often sees the darkness clearly, but seeing is not the same as acting. His sarcasm lets him survive emotionally, but it also allows him to pretend he is less involved than he is. The violence strips that illusion away.

**Likes and Interests:** Manuel likes dark humor, music, cynicism as a lifestyle accessory, and intellectual distance. He pretends not to care because caring has teeth and he has apparently decided dental drama is enough.

**Key Relationships:** Manuel belongs to Alex, Marcos, Mario, and Enrique’s orbit, though he often comments from the shadows. He is close enough to know the truth of the Lourdes plot, and his confession later becomes part of what cracks the group open. María and Mónika visiting him in the hospital shows that beneath the sarcasm, Manuel matters deeply to the group.

**Character Arc:** Manuel begins as the sarcastic observer who joins the revenge plot while pretending emotional distance will protect him. It does not. After being attacked, he breaks down and admits the fear underneath the cynicism. His arc strips away the armor. The boy who joked about everything is forced to tell the truth out loud.

**Content Use:** Manuel is strong for posts about sarcasm, emotional repression, trauma humor, reluctant vulnerability, and the collapse of defensive detachment. He works beautifully for quote cards with dark humor and hidden sadness.

## **Fernando Pepino**

**Role:** Fernando is the former best friend, the bully, the secret wound, and one of Book 1’s most loaded emotional landmines.

**Visual Identity:** Fernando is stylish, intimidating, youthful, and expensive-looking. He is usually seen with a baseball cap or similar headwear, often with a lollipop, and he carries himself with arrogant confidence. Even his props feel like weapons: cap, lollipop, smirk, exit wound. He should look fashionable and dangerous without seeming older than the other students.

**School Uniform:** Fernando wears the Excelsior Academy uniform but bends it toward his own identity. Rolled sleeves, cap, accessories, and attitude make him look like he is technically complying while emotionally mocking the administration. He turns uniformity into provocation.

**Outside School Style:** Fernando’s outside-school style is designer-heavy: polos, high-end streetwear hoodies, dark jeans, fitted slacks, luxury sneakers or boots, strong cologne, expensive watch, and occasional chain. He looks like money learned how to threaten people.

**Personality and Story Function:** Fernando is cruel, defensive, wounded, provocative, funny, and secretly vulnerable. Early in the book, he is introduced as arrogant and hostile, especially toward Alex. His cruelty is real, but it is also a mask. The story gradually reveals that he and Alex were once inseparable, and the collapse of that friendship left behind something ugly, unresolved, and combustible.

Fernando’s function in Book 1 is to complicate the reader’s moral map. He begins as someone easy to dislike, but the book gradually exposes the wound underneath the behavior. He is not just “the bully.” He is a character shaped by rejection, shame, secrecy, desire, anger, and betrayal.

**Likes and Interests:** Fernando likes status, provocation, control, self-protection, shock value, and performance. His senior-year B-roll answer to Ricardo is outrageous, which fits him perfectly because Fernando hides discomfort behind spectacle.

**Key Relationships:** Fernando’s most important relationship is with Alex, his former best friend and emotional fracture point. Their broken bond shapes much of the emotional architecture of the book. His later alliance with Marcos is unexpected but crucial because Marcos sees past the bully mask and recognizes a shared wound. Fernando’s relationship with the broader group is hostile, but not simple. He is the walking consequence of something unresolved.

**Character Arc:** Fernando’s arc moves from antagonist to revealed wound to something far more dangerous. Internally, Book 1 positions him as both victim and weapon. The later reveal that he has been inhabiting the role of the Grim Cojuelo gives his arc full horror: Fernando does not simply wear the mask. He becomes the monster the group’s silence, rejection, humiliation, and rage helped create.

**Content Use:** Use Fernando carefully. Spoiler-safe content can frame him as Alex’s former best friend, stylish antagonist, bully, and emotionally loaded rival. Internal or spoiler-heavy content can explore his connection to the Grim Cojuelo, his alliance with Marcos, and the way unresolved pain becomes monstrosity.

She survives horror, questions Alex, supports the truth, and refuses to let other people’s guilt become her emotional responsibility. Her arc is about agency. She stops being the prize in other people’s rivalry and becomes a witness to the cost of their choices.

**Content Use:** Melissa is ideal for posts about survivorhood, emotional intelligence, romantic tension, agency, grief, witnesshood, and the difference between being loved and being understood.

She represents the kind of cruelty people excuse until someone responds with cruelty of their own. Her arc is the first domino in Book 1’s moral collapse.

**Content Use:** Lourdes is ideal for content about institutional cruelty, abusive teachers, revenge triggers, power, humiliation, and the danger of mistaking fear for respect.

## **The Grim Cojuelo**

**Role:** The Grim Cojuelo is the masked killer, Dominican nightmare figure, and physical manifestation of revenge turned theatrical.

**Visual Identity:** The Grim Cojuelo appears as a tall hooded figure in a black robe, carrying a curved scythe. At the pier, it is described as a deathly figure with a grotesque mask and glowing red eyes, arriving at the exact moment Enrique and Melissa’s romantic fantasy peaks. The visual should combine Grim Reaper menace with Dominican Diablo Cojuelo influence without turning into generic skeleton cosplay.

**Costume and Symbolism:** The Grim Cojuelo’s identity should evoke dark robes, mask, horns, scythe, carnival-horror symbolism, and a presence designed to turn guilt into spectacle. The look should feel Dominican, cinematic, and horrifying. No bones, no cheap Halloween nonsense. This is not a seasonal discount reaper. This is guilt with a cultural memory and a blade.

**Personality and Story Function:** The Grim Cojuelo is silent when silence is more terrifying, theatrical when control is the goal, and cruel when punishment needs an audience. The killer weaponizes phones, videos, livestreams, fear, and exposure. In Book 1, the Grim Cojuelo is not merely killing people. It is producing horror as content.

The Grim Cojuelo’s function is to make guilt visible. The mask targets the group symbolically rather than randomly. It turns private sins into public nightmare and forces the characters to watch consequences unfold.

**Likes and Interests:** Punishment, performance, fear, spectacle, exposure, revenge, and making the group confront what they buried. Truly the worst event planner in Santo Domingo.

**Key Relationships:** The Grim Cojuelo is tied to the entire friend group through guilt, silence, old wounds, and revenge. Its targets are not arbitrary. The mask becomes the story’s judgment machine.

**Character Arc:** The Grim Cojuelo begins as a shadow watching Melissa and Enrique, then becomes an active predator, then a full social nightmare. Internally, the reveal that Fernando is behind the mask, with Marcos involved in the orchestration, turns the killer from external monster into the group’s own history returning with a scythe. Public-facing material should never reveal this.

**Content Use:** Use the Grim Cojuelo for horror branding, Dominican folklore positioning, teaser copy, cover language, masked-killer suspense, and thematic posts about guilt, punishment, revenge, and spectacle.

# **Core Character Dynamics in Mortal Vengeance**

The emotional engine of *Mortal Vengeance* is not simply “killer attacks teenagers,” because that would be cute but basic. The real engine is complicity.

Alex manipulates because control makes him feel safe. Marcos burns because injustice makes restraint feel like cowardice. Mario hesitates because belonging matters more than his own moral alarm system. Manuel hides behind sarcasm because caring hurts. Enrique wants to please and becomes useful to the wrong person. Melissa becomes the ideal everyone projects onto until she forces herself back into the story as a person. Mónika performs confidence while absorbing fear. María protects until she becomes a target. Fernando turns old pain into monstrosity. Lourdes abuses power until the abused become dangerous. Ricardo tries to restore justice after the kids have already poisoned the ecosystem.

The nasty little machine at the center of Book 1 is this: everyone thinks they are reacting to someone else’s cruelty, but every reaction adds another blade to the wheel.

# **Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale**

## **Book 0 Character Spotlight**

*Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale* is the prequel to *Mortal Vengeance*, but it should not be treated as a decorative origin story or lore appendix with trauma wallpaper. It is the how-dunnit behind the nightmare. It traces the institutional rot, religious abuse, silence, guilt, class pressure, and psychological collapse that make the later killings feel inevitable.

This book is less concerned with “who is killing people?” and more concerned with “who built the monster, brick by brick, then acted shocked when it learned interior design?” The answer is not one person. It is an institution, a culture, a family system, a theology of shame, and a student body trained to survive by looking away.

The book frames the Grim Cojuelo as the moment folklore becomes psychology, psychology becomes accusation, and accusation becomes identity. The mask does not appear from nowhere. It is assembled through whispers, cruelty, silence, fear, shame, and adult failure.

## **Julián Díaz**

**Visual Identity:** Julián is a seventeen-year-old senior at Excelsior Academy. **Likes and Interests:** Julián likes drawing, folklore, dark legends, the Grim Cojuelo, symbolism, hidden meanings, music, intellectual spaces, and any form of expression that lets him say what Excelsior forces him to swallow.

Padre Ángel and Sister María Gracia represent the institution’s predatory core. Lourdes becomes one of his most consistent sources of humiliation. His parents, Doña Patricia and Don Ramón, represent the painful collision between love, sacrifice, faith, poverty, and shame.

**Character Arc:** Julián begins as a haunted student trying to survive Excelsior’s machinery of guilt. His art gives him a voice. Then his voice becomes graffiti. Then graffiti becomes accusation. Then accusation becomes mythology. By the end, Julián’s tragedy becomes evidence, legacy, and ignition. His story does not simply explain the Grim Cojuelo. It shows how silence manufactures monsters, then files paperwork pretending not to know where they came from.

**Content Use:** Julián is central for posts about religious trauma, institutional abuse, guilt, art, folklore, psychological collapse, and the origin of the Grim Cojuelo. Public-safe content should focus on his descent and the school’s failure without revealing the full final sequence.

# **Mortal Vengeance II: To Reel or Not Too Real?**

## **Book 2 Character Spotlight**

*Mortal Vengeance II: To Reel or Not Too Real?* is the direct sequel to *Mortal Vengeance*. Three years after the Excelsior Academy scandal, the surviving students have become public figures, trauma symbols, and reality-TV cast members on *The Real Scholars of Excelsior Academy*. The horror has evolved. In Book 1, the nightmare lived inside the school. In *A Grim Tale*, it lived inside the institution. In Book 2, it lives inside the camera.

This book is not simply “the survivors get hunted again.” That would be efficient, but basic. Book 2 is about what happens when survival becomes marketable, grief becomes branding, and every unresolved wound gets dragged under studio lighting. The killer still matters, obviously. This is not a wellness retreat with confessionals. But the real monster is bigger than the mask. It is the producer, the edit, the audience, the contract, the network, the gossip cycle, and the appetite to watch damaged people collapse beautifully.

Book 2’s character engine is **exploitation**. Alex wants reinvention. Melissa wants ownership of her life. Mónika wants control of the narrative. Mario wants everyone to stop emotionally exploding in public, which is adorable and doomed. Lucía wants to tell the truth on her own terms. Shane wants ratings. Ricardo wants grief to stop becoming entertainment. The new cast wants relevance. The old sins want a comeback tour.

The result is a sequel where everyone is performing, hiding, confessing, lying, healing, spiraling, flirting, grieving, and possibly dying in 4K. Premium trauma package. Horrible subscription model.

# **Core Character Dynamics in Mortal Vengeance II**

The emotional engine of *Mortal Vengeance II: To Reel or Not Too Real?* is exploitation.

Alex wants to become someone better, but the show wants the old version of him because the old version gets ratings. Melissa wants to own her life, but everyone keeps turning her into a symbol. Mónika knows how to perform, but now performance is a battlefield. Mario wants peace, but peace is useless to a producer with a call sheet. Lucía returns with truth, and the world immediately tries to monetize it. Ricardo carries grief too real for television, which naturally means television wants it anyway. Shane sees everyone’s wounds and starts arranging furniture around them.

The new cast members sharpen the machinery. Rafa performs masculinity because attention rewards him. Valeria negotiates villainy because opportunity has a price. Camila treats cruelty like labor because she understands the contract better than the ethics. Elías drags old entitlement into new spectacle. Doña Silvia and Doña Yvelisse fight from the family perimeter, trying to protect their children from both love and cameras. Don Rolando turns family trauma into public disaster. Humberto carries inherited grief. Father Ángel, Sister María Gracia, and Beltrán prove that the original institution may have collapsed, but its sins are still billing overtime.

That is Book 2’s nasty little machine: the survivors escaped the massacre, but they did not escape the story. And now the story has producers, confessionals, sponsors, drones, edits, and a killer who understands that nothing spreads faster than fear with good footage.

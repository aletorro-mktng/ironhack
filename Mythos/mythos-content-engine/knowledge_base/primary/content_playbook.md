# Content Playbook
**Alejandro Torres**

---

## Why this playbook exists
This playbook defines how content is planned, produced, distributed, measured, and improved under my leadership. It is designed to align **editorial quality, brand coherence, and performance** across channels and teams.


---

## 1) Objectives & North Star

Every piece of content must serve at least one of these objectives:

* **Brand authority** (credibility, trust, differentiation)
* **Organic growth** (SEO, discoverability, long-tail value)
* **Performance enablement** (assets that can scale in paid)
* **Community value** (engagement, saves, shares, retention)

If a piece does not clearly support an objective, it does not get produced.

**Operationally:**
Before green‑lighting any content, I require the objective to be written in one sentence at the top of the brief. If the team cannot articulate the objective clearly, the topic goes back to the backlog. This single step eliminates vanity content and keeps production aligned with business outcomes.

---

## 2) Audience & Intent

I plan content around **intent**, not formats.

For each topic, we define:

* Who it is for (primary / secondary audience)
* What problem they are trying to solve
* What decision stage they are in (discover, consider, decide)

Formats are chosen *after* intent is clear.

**Tactical application:**
I document intent explicitly in the brief (one paragraph, no more). This avoids format‑driven requests like “we need a Reel” and replaces them with intent‑driven decisions like “this topic needs fast visual proof” or “this requires depth and search visibility.”

**Example:**
A long-form SEO article for Badi addressed *landlords trying to rent faster* — not “people interested in decoration.” That intent justified educational depth, lifestyle tone, and a commercial CTA without feeling salesy.

A similar approach was applied in LUCA Foods, where trend-led social content was only produced when it could clearly connect cultural relevance with product education. Posts that couldn’t bridge that gap were deprioritized, regardless of short-term reach potential.

---

## 3) Editorial Framework (Default Flow)
Unless there is a strong reason to deviate, content follows this structure:
1. **Problem / Tension** – A real, recognisable pain point
2. **Context / Education** – Why it happens and why it matters
3. **Solution** – The brand, product, or idea as a logical answer
4. **CTA** – One clear next step

This applies to blog posts, videos, emails, and social pieces.

---

## 4) Channel Strategy

I do not treat channels equally. Each channel has a job, and trade‑offs are intentional.

### Blog / SEO
* Evergreen > news
* One clear search intent per piece
* Structured headings, internal linking, scannability
* Written to be understood on first read

**My stance:** I prioritize fewer, stronger evergreen pieces over volume. A blog post is an asset, not a post.

### Social (Organic)
* First 2–3 seconds decide everything
* One idea per post
* Built to be saved or shared

**My stance:** I prefer fewer posts with a clear hook over daily noise. Repurposing is allowed only if the content feels native to the platform.

### Video (Vertical‑first)
* Scripted for rhythm, not polish
* Subtitles are mandatory
* Clarity beats production value

**My stance:** Speed and clarity outperform cinematic quality. If a message works without sound, it works.

### Email
* One message per send
* Clear value exchange
* CTA matched to funnel stage

**My stance:** Email is not a dumping ground for content. Every send must earn its place in the inbox.

---

## 5) Performance & Paid Alignment
Content is created to **enable performance**, even when it starts organic.

With paid teams, I align on:
- Hooks and angles to test
- Naming conventions and UTMs
- Variants and formats
- What content is worth scaling

We learn from what performs and feed that insight back into editorial planning.

---

## 6) Production Workflow (Process)

This is how work moves from idea to publication:

1. Topic & intent definition
2. Brief (objective, audience, format, CTA)
3. Production (writing, video, visuals)
4. QA (brand, clarity, rights)
5. Distribution
6. Measurement & learnings

Speed matters, but **quality gates are not optional**..

---

## 7) Measurement & Dashboard

Metrics only matter if they inform decisions.

Core metrics I track:
* Reach & growth
* Engagement (likes, comments, saves, shares)
* Video retention
* CTR and traffic contribution
* Content activation in paid

**How I use them:**
I review performance on a fixed cadence (weekly for tactical signals, monthly for strategic trends). A pivot is triggered when a pattern repeats across formats or channels—not by single-post outliers. I separate signal from noise by looking for consistency: what people watch through, save, click, or reuse.

If a piece performs well organically, it becomes a candidate for paid testing. If paid assets underperform repeatedly, the insight feeds back into editorial planning—not just creative tweaks.

Metrics inform decisions, not vanity reporting.

---

## 8) Governance & Quality Control (Standards)

Governance defines *what good looks like*.

Before publishing, I verify:
* Brand coherence (visual & verbal)
* Rights and usage permissions
* Clarity, structure, and factual accuracy
* CTA relevance and correctness

Process moves content forward. Governance decides if it ships.

If it does not meet brand standards, it does not go out.


---

## Final Principle
Content is not output.

Content is **strategy made visible**.

If it does not inform, position, or move someone to act, it is not finished.

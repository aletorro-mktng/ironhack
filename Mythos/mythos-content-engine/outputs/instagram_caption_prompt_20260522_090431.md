## brand_voice

# Brand Voice

## Purpose

This file defines the voice for Mythos content, especially promotional and editorial material for the Mortal Vengeance series.

The voice should make every caption, post, blog, newsletter blurb, and character spotlight feel like it belongs to the same dark narrative world. Content should sound specific, cinematic, and emotionally charged, not like generic book marketing.

## Core Voice

Mythos speaks with controlled intensity.

The voice is:

- Dark but not melodramatic.
- Cinematic but not overwritten.
- Mythic but grounded in human emotion.
- Sharp, direct, and suspenseful.
- Emotionally intimate without becoming sentimental.
- Confident without sounding arrogant.
- Mysterious without becoming vague.

The writing should feel like a door opening into danger. It should invite the audience to step closer, but it should never explain everything too neatly.

## Brand Personality

Mythos is the voice of a storyteller who understands revenge, grief, power, betrayal, loyalty, and consequence.

It does not beg for attention. It creates tension.

It does not overexplain the story. It reveals just enough to make the reader want the next piece.

It does not flatten characters into tropes. It treats them as people shaped by wounds, choices, and impossible debts.

## Emotional Register

Prioritize these emotions:

- Tension
- Obsession
- Dread
- Defiance
- Grief
- Moral conflict
- Dangerous desire
- Loyalty under pressure
- The cost of revenge
- The weight of old choices

Avoid emotions that feel too bright, casual, or detached unless the specific platform requires a lighter hook.

## Tone Pillars

### 1. Cinematic Tension

Write like each line could be the opening of a scene.

Good:

- "Some debts are paid in blood. Others keep collecting interest."
- "He came home for answers. The city gave him a body count."
- "Every secret in this family has a pulse."

Avoid:

- "This exciting story is full of twists and turns."
- "Readers will love this thrilling adventure."
- "Get ready for an amazing journey."

### 2. Moral Pressure

Revenge should never feel simple. Every act should carry a cost.

Good:

- "Revenge does not heal the wound. It teaches it to speak."
- "Justice has rules. Vengeance has memory."
- "He wants the truth. What he finds may deserve to stay buried."

Avoid:

- "Revenge is awesome."
- "The hero gets payback."
- "Bad guys better watch out."

### 3. Mythic Weight

Use mythic language sparingly. The world can feel ancient, cursed, or fated, but the prose should remain clear.

Good:

- "The past does not die here. It waits."
- "Every family has ghosts. His learned how to hunt."
- "Blood remembers what people try to forget."

Avoid:

- "In a realm of epic destiny and legendary darkness..."
- "A timeless saga of cosmic proportions..."
- "The ultimate battle between good and evil begins..."

### 4. Character-First Darkness

The darkness should come from character choices, secrets, and consequences, not empty shock value.

Good:

- "She knows where the bodies are buried because she chose the ground."
- "He is not afraid of dying. He is afraid the truth will make him deserve it."
- "They loved each other once. That is why the betrayal cuts so deep."

Avoid:

- Gore for gore's sake.
- Villains described as evil without motivation.
- Trauma used only as decoration.

## Style Rules

- Prefer short, charged sentences.
- Use sentence fragments when they create rhythm or impact.
- Keep paragraphs lean.
- Lead with conflict, consequence, or mystery.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs.
- Let imagery do some of the work.
- Avoid generic hype words.
- Avoid explaining the entire premise in one block.
- End social captions with tension, a question, or a clean call to action.

## Point of View

Default brand voice should use third person or direct address.

Use third person when describing the story, characters, or world:

- "He wanted justice. What he found was older than justice."
- "She built her life on silence. Now the truth wants payment."

Use direct address when inviting the audience into a post:

- "Choose carefully. In this family, every answer costs something."
- "If revenge had a voice, whose name would it say first?"

Avoid first person unless the content is explicitly written as author commentary or a character voice piece.

## Pacing

The voice should move like suspense:

1. Hook with danger, contradiction, or emotional pressure.
2. Reveal one specific detail.
3. Raise the stakes.
4. Leave a question, image, or consequence unresolved.

Example structure:

```text
He came looking for the man who ruined his life.
He found a family secret instead.
Now every name on his list points back home.
```

## Platform Adaptation

### Instagram

Instagram captions should be cinematic, polished, and emotionally hook-driven.

- Start with a strong line.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use a small number of relevant hashtags only when needed.
- End with a question or simple CTA.

Example:

```text
Some families pass down heirlooms.
This one passed down a war.

What would you do if the truth about your bloodline demanded revenge?
```

### TikTok

TikTok content should be sharper, faster, and more hook-heavy.

- Use punchy openings.
- Create immediate curiosity.
- Make the premise easy to understand in seconds.
- Favor contrast and emotional stakes.

Example:

```text
POV: You came home to bury the past.
The past was waiting with a knife.
```

### Blog Posts

Blog posts should be atmospheric but clear.

- Use strong section openings.
- Explain themes without draining mystery.
- Connect craft, character, and audience appeal.
- Avoid academic stiffness.

### Newsletter Blurbs

Newsletter blurbs should feel intimate, direct, and slightly conspiratorial.

- Make the reader feel close to the story.
- Offer a glimpse behind the curtain.
- Keep the tone warm but still aligned with the darker brand.

### Character Spotlights

Character spotlights should focus on wound, want, contradiction, and consequence.

Use this pattern:

- What they want.
- What they hide.
- What they fear.
- What they might become.

### Review Pull Quotes

Pull quotes should be short, intense, and clean.

Good:

- "A revenge story with teeth."
- "Dark, precise, and impossible to look away from."
- "Every secret lands like a blade."

Avoid:

- Long, crowded quotes.
- Quotes that reveal plot twists.
- Quotes that sound like generic praise.

## Content Dos

- Do create mystery without confusion.
- Do make stakes personal.
- Do use sensory details.
- Do connect revenge to consequence.
- Do keep the language elegant but readable.
- Do make each post feel tied to a specific character, conflict, or theme.
- Do preserve unanswered questions.

## Content Don'ts

- Do not summarize the whole story too plainly.
- Do not use cheerful hype language.
- Do not make revenge sound consequence-free.
- Do not overuse hashtags or emojis.
- Do not rely on vague phrases like "dark secrets" without a specific image or angle.
- Do not flatten the series into generic thriller, fantasy, or action copy.
- Do not promise content that the story cannot deliver.

## Reusable Voice Patterns

Use these patterns as inspiration, not as fixed copy.

```text
He wanted [simple goal].
He found [dangerous truth].
Now [personal consequence].
```

```text
In [place/family/world], [ordinary thing] is never just [ordinary thing].
It is [threat/consequence/memory].
```

```text
Some people inherit [object/status/name].
[Character] inherited [burden/conflict/debt].
```

```text
The question is not whether [character] will survive.
The question is what survival will turn them into.
```

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing or saving generated content, check:

- Does it sound specific to Mortal Vengeance?
- Does it contain a clear emotional hook?
- Does it create tension within the first line?
- Does it avoid generic book marketing language?
- Does it preserve mystery instead of overexplaining?
- Does it connect revenge, justice, or truth to consequence?
- Does it fit the platform format?
- Could this line belong to any thriller, or only to this world?

# Mortal Vengeance Series Overview

Mortal Vengeance is a thriller and horror series by Alejandro Torres De la Rocha.

The series blends revenge, psychological suspense, Caribbean Gothic atmosphere, dark academia, Dominican folklore, institutional corruption, media spectacle, and morally complicated characters.

The series includes:

- Mortal Vengeance
- Mortal Vengeance: A Grim Tale
- Mortal Vengeance II: To Reel or Not Too Real?

The series is built around trauma, silence, guilt, survival, revenge, truth, and the cost of exposing what powerful people would rather bury.

## Core Series Identity

Mortal Vengeance is not a generic slasher series.

It combines:

- Psychological thriller tension
- Slasher horror structure
- Caribbean Gothic atmosphere
- Dominican folklore
- Dark academia
- Revenge tragedy
- Media satire
- Social and institutional critique

The horror does not only come from killers, blood, or bodies. It also comes from schools, families, reputations, silence, pressure, and the systems that protect themselves before they protect people.

## The Grim Cojuelo

A central mythic figure in the series is the Grim Cojuelo.

The Grim Cojuelo connects Dominican folklore, punishment, spectacle, guilt, and institutional rot. It is not just a monster or killer image. It represents the way buried wrongs return, the way guilt collects interest, and the way silence eventually demands payment.

## Marketing Positioning

Marketing content should position the series as:

- Caribbean Gothic horror
- Dark YA psychological thriller
- Slasher thriller
- Revenge thriller
- Dark academia horror
- Dominican folklore horror
- Media spectacle thriller

The content should emphasize the series’ specific cultural identity, sharp voice, morally complicated characters, and mix of horror, drama, satire, and emotional fallout.

## What to Avoid

Avoid positioning the series as:

- Generic horror
- Generic YA thriller
- Simple revenge fantasy
- Basic serial killer fiction
- Standard dark academia without cultural specificity
- A story where vengeance is clean or consequence-free

Mortal Vengeance should always feel specific, dangerous, emotional, and rooted in a world where every secret has teeth.
